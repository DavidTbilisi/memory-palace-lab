# Live lecture — semantic listening, notes, and delayed encoding

How to **keep the thread** while a talk is still in the air, **take notes that stay honest under recall** (when you *can* write), and **hand off** to palace / Anki only **after** comprehension — without pretending you can Major-encode every sentence in real time. For **friends, bars, and walking-and-talking** with **no notebook**, see **§9**.

**Prerequisites:** `comprehension-protocol.md`, `confusion-triage.md`, `mind-palace.md`, `retrieval-protocol.md`. Optional: `navigator.md` (study sprint after class), `heuristic-palace.md` (when you are stuck mid-stream).

**Book spine:** This bundles the book’s **`# Active Listening`** (STREAM skeleton) and **`# Semantic Reading`** (tag information by **function**, not only wording). Same moves — reading uses the eyes; lectures use the ears.

## 1. Hard constraints (say them up front)

- **Working memory is tiny** compared to lecture bandwidth. If you try to *store* and *decorate* at full eloquence, you will drop the **structure** first — and structure is what lets you rebuild details later.
- **Goal for the room:** a **recoverable skeleton** within **24 hours**, not a transcript. Verbatim belongs to **recordings**, not to hand cramps.
- **Encoding rule:** treat the lecture like **Gate 1–4 live**, and treat **Gate 5 + mnemonics** as **after-class work** unless you are doing a **deliberate** 3–5 item “micro-peg” drill (see §5).

---

## 2. Semantic listening = semantic reading with your ears

**Semantic** here means: you track **what job each chunk does** in the argument, not every syllable.

While the speaker moves, ask the **same question** the book asks in *Semantic Reading*: *what kind of information is this?* Route each incoming chunk to a **small tag set** in the **margin** (one letter or symbol). That keeps attention on **structure** instead of on copying slides.

**Reuse the book’s lightweight grammar** (see **`beating-the-red-queen.md`** — **`# Semantic Reading`** — table of `N` name, `D` date, `P` place, `R` relation/cause, `L` delay, `C` constraint, `B` bottleneck, `M` measure, `Def` definition, `Q` question, `A` action). In the hall you only need **5–7** tags active at once; add the rest when the course demands it.

**Listen-specific additions:**

| Tag | When you hear… |
|-----|------------------|
| `T` | **Topic shift** — “now a different sub-problem” (start a new sub-head in notes) |
| `Ex` | **Worked example / story** — long surface text; capture **one** outcome line only |
| `Σ` | **Summary / punchline** — often exam-load-bearing |

**Bridge to STREAM (Active Listening):** in parallel, keep a **3–7 topic ledger** and a **thread order** (Subjects + Thread in STREAM). Margin tags say **what** the chunk is; the topic ledger says **where** it belongs. Assertions (`A` in STREAM sense) and Measures (`M`) pair naturally with `R` + `M` margin tags.

## 3. One-page note template (paper or tablet)

Divide the sheet **before** the bell:

| Zone | Job |
|------|-----|
| **Top strip (2 lines)** | **Title**, date, **prerequisite guess** (“needs linear algebra”) — *Gate 1 Locate* |
| **Left rail** | **Topic numbers** `T1 … T7` down the page; every note line starts by pointing at one |
| **Main column** | **One line per idea max**; start with a **margin tag** (`Def`, `R`, `Ex`…) then **keywords only** |
| **Right rail** | **`Q` only** — genuine holes (“why is this legal?”); **vocabulary** you will define after class |
| **Footer (3 lines, post-bell)** | **NEDF micro:** **N** talk title · **E** one-sentence essence · **F** one way you are likely to **misremember** this week |

**Anti-patterns:**

- **Decorative highlighting** with no tag — you feel productive but lose **searchability**.
- **Transcribing slides** the instructor will publish anyway — you pay **attention tax** twice.
- **No `Q` column** — confusion hides until the exam; use `confusion-triage.md` after class on those marks.

---

## 4. Minute-by-minute playbook (while listening)

**0–2 min — freeze the map**

- Write **two sentences**: “This lecture is a **[category]** about **[function]** for **[who/course]].” If you cannot, you are still lost at **Locate** — listen extra-hard for the framing sentence, or mark **`Q`** and catch the TA after.

**Steady state — semantic routing**

- On each **new idea**, decide **topic bucket** (`T3` …) then **function tag** (`Def`, `R`, …) then **≤12 words** unless it is a **definition** you must quote.

**When density spikes**

- **Drop detail**, keep **relation**: `R: A → B` arrow notation beats a paragraph.
- If the speaker chains **three examples**, keep **one** and tag `Ex×3 → see recording 42:00` if allowed.

**When you are cognitively stuck**

- Do **not** fight for full understanding live. Mark **`Q`** and **one keyword**; optionally park a **Heuristic Palace** “Classify” move after class (`heuristic-palace.md`).

**What not to do in the room**

- Do **not** build a **full mind palace** for the whole lecture during speech. Palace = **slow**, **spatial**, **post-hoc** container (`mind-palace.md`). In-room, you only maintain **addressability** (topic + tag + keyword).

---

## 5. Micro-pegs (optional, 3–5 items max)

If the instructor says “**this is the exam’s contract**” or gives an immovable **formula / definition**, you *may* assign **one** vivid hook per **hour** on a **pre-memorized** short peg list (body list, ring on watch, corners of the desk). **Stop at five new pegs** or you will lose the thread.

Everything else waits for **§6**.

---

## 6. First ten minutes after class (non-negotiable repair)

This is where memory systems earn their pay.

1. **Blank-paper regeneration** — close notes; write **topic thread + definitions + one graph**. Partial is fine. (**Gate 5** probe.)
2. **Diff against notes** — open sheet; **ink only** what you missed (red pen rule).
3. **Encode decision** — pick **at most** **5–15** Anki cards or **one short palace corridor** for the whole session (`retrieval-protocol.md`). Long lectures → **chunk by topic**, not by minute.

If step 1 is empty, you did not have a lecture in memory — you had **stenography practice**. Shrink live notes next time and **raise** `Q` courage.

---

## 7. NEDF for the *method* (meta)

- **N:** Semantic lecture capture + delayed encoding
- **E:** Tags + topic ledger during talk; regeneration + cards after
- **D:** Not full comprehension protocol at home on cold text only — this is **time-pressured input**
- **F:** Confusing transcript-like pages with **no** skeleton — fails **Regenerate** the same day

## 9. Friend monologues and “unconnected” stories (no notes)

Same job as **Active Listening** in the book: unstructured speech — but **social**, **polite**, and **paperless**. You are not grading them; you are **building a skeleton so the emotional point does not drown in detours**.

### 9.1 What you hold in memory (silent STREAM)

You cannot write; you **compress** to **at most five movable handles** (say them internally in one breath each):

| Handle | Question (one word each) |
|--------|---------------------------|
| **S** | What **buckets**? (usually **2–4**, rarely 5) |
| **T** | What **order** are we visiting buckets? (even if time jumps) |
| **R** | **Who** matters — who did / felt / wanted what? |
| **E** | **Spine** — situation → tension → turn → outcome (skip lesson unless they offer it) |
| **Σ** | If I had to **text someone one sentence**, what is this **for** them right now? |

**Semantic listening still applies:** you are not tracking syllables; you are tracking **function** — *complaint vs memory vs plan vs joke vs ask-for-advice*. Mis-classify and you will answer the wrong *kind* of thing.

### 9.2 Tiny loop-backs (structure without interrogation)

Every **natural pause** (breath, laugh, “anyway…”), you may **deposit** one **non-performative** line:

- **Mirror + slot:** “So **X** happened, then **Y** — is that the part that’s eating you?” (checks **E** + **R**)
- **Punchline check:** “Sounds like the real sting is **Z**?” (targets **Σ**)
- **Permission to ramble:** “Keep going — I’m with you.” (when **R** is clear and they need flow, not dissecting)

**Failure mode:** turning their story into **your** lecture (“What you should do is…”) before **Σ** is stable. **Repair:** one honest question that only **names** the tension, not the solution.

### 9.3 When the thread feels “random”

Detours are usually **emotion-first**, not **logic-first**. Tactics:

- **Anchor on people, not timestamps** — “your boss / your sister / past-you” beats “Tuesday then Thursday.”
- **Let contradictions sit** — note internally `Q`; ask once after a pause: “Earlier you said A — does it still feel true next to B?”
- **One invisible peg trick** — press **finger joints** 1–4 = four topics you will **release** after the walk; no one sees it. Stop at four or you drop empathy.

### 9.4 After you part (optional 30 seconds)

In the elevator / stairwell: **retell the spine once** in **one whispered sentence** each for **S, R, Σ**. No Anki — this is **relationship memory**, not coursework. If something mattered for **you** (advice owed, date promised), **one** phone note or calendar line *then*.
