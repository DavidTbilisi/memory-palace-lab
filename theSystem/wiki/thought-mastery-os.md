# Thought Mastery OS

**Summary**: Extension of metacognition from *monitoring* (am I thinking well?) toward *intervention* (what do I do about a bad thought right now?). Imports CBT-style thought-records as a NEDF deck of named cognitive distortions and runs each catch + counter as a SPEAR procedure. **Canonical owner of the cognitive-distortion taxonomy** as of 2026-08-31, now carrying Leahy's worry-specific cards alongside the Beck/Burns core.

## Core Claim

```
Thought mastery = (Catch distortion) → (Name it) → (Write counter) — under 60s
```

metacognition tells you *something is off* with your thinking. CBT-style thought-records tell you:

1. **What kind** of off (which named distortion)
2. **What to substitute** (the counter, anchored on evidence, not on hope)

Recognition is the gate; substitution is the move. Both are drillable. Substituting *without* naming usually produces toxic positivity, not re-frame.

## The Distortion Deck (15 NEDF Cards)

The cognitive distortions from the CBT literature, encoded as a [NEDF](./nedf-overview.md) deck. **Cards 1–10** are the Beck/Burns core. **Cards 11–15** are the worry-specific extension from Leahy's Table 6.3 (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005) — same deck, same slots, same drill ladder; they are added below, not run as a second list.

| Distortion | The disguise it wears | The give-away |
|---|---|---|
| **Catastrophizing** | "Realistic planning" | The worst case is given disproportionate probability mass |
| **Mind-reading** | "Reading the room" | Confident claims about another's internal state without check-back evidence |
| **Personalization** | "Taking responsibility" | Attributing outcomes to self that have multi-causal sources |
| **All-or-nothing** | "High standards" | Categorical language ("always", "never", "ruined", "perfect") |
| **Over-generalization** | "Spotting patterns" | One data point lifted to a rule |
| **Mental filter** | "Being realistic" | Only the negative evidence is considered; the positive is invisible |
| **Disqualifying-positive** | "Humility" | Positive evidence is dismissed by category ("they were just being nice") |
| **Emotional reasoning** | "Trusting your gut" | "I feel X, therefore X is true" — feeling treated as data about the world |
| **Should-statements** | "Holding standards" | Rigid rule-claims unanchored to a chosen value or commitment |
| **Labeling** | "Self-knowledge" | A single act extended to global identity ("I failed" → "I'm a failure") |
| **Fortune-telling** | "Forecasting" | A future *event* asserted as fact — "I'll fail that exam", "I won't get the job" (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005) |
| **What-if cascade** | "Being thorough" | A chain of questions where each answer spawns the next one and no answer satisfies — "Yeah, but what if…" (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005) |
| **Unfair comparison** | "Benchmarking" | The comparison set is only people who did better; the standard recedes as you approach it (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005) |
| **Regret orientation** | "Learning from mistakes" | A past decision judged against information that arrived after it — "I could have had a better job if I had tried" (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005) |
| **Inability to disconfirm** | "Vigilance" | Every counter-evidence is pre-absorbed; nothing you could observe would end the worry (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005) |

Card numbers used elsewhere on this page refer to row order in the table above — 1 = Catastrophizing, 6 = Mental filter, 7 = Disqualifying-positive, 11 = Fortune-telling, 15 = Inability to disconfirm.

Each card carries the standard NEDF slots:

- **Name-hook** — a vivid concrete scene where this distortion looks like *itself* in your life
- **Essence** — one-sentence read of what the distortion is doing
- **Distinguisher** — the closest confusable distortion (Catastrophizing vs Emotional-reasoning is the canonical hard pair — both feel like prediction; Catastrophizing predicts events, Emotional-reasoning predicts truth)
- **Failure** — the disguise above (the bait the distortion wears so it isn't caught)

Two distortions usually co-fire. Label the *primary*; note the secondary.

### Cards 11–15 — the worry-specific extension

Leahy's Step Three ("Challenge Your Worried Thinking") supplies five cards the Beck/Burns core did not carry as separate entries (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). They earn their slots because each one cuts a *different* pathology out of what the old deck was labelling "catastrophizing".

The five sort by **which tense the worry is hiding in** — an arrangement of this wiki's, not Leahy's:

```
                        ┌──────────────────────────────┐
                        │ 11 FORTUNE-TELLING           │
                        │    future · one event        │
                        └───────────────┬──────────────┘
             ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
  ┌──────────┴───────────────┐                          ┌──────────┴───────────────┐
  │ 15 INABILITY TO          │                          │ 12 WHAT-IF CASCADE       │
  │    DISCONFIRM            │                          │    future · branching    │
  │    no tense · unfalsif.  │                          │    chain                 │
  └──────────┬───────────────┘                          └──────────┬───────────────┘
             │                                                     │
  ┌──────────┴───────────────┐                          ┌──────────┴───────────────┐
  │ 14 REGRET ORIENTATION    │                          │ 13 UNFAIR COMPARISON     │
  │    past · wrong info set │──────────────────────────│    present · wrong ref   │
  └──────────────────────────┘                          └──────────────────────────┘
```

Five cards, five corners ([representation-rules](./representation-rules.md) Rule 10) — an empty corner means you are only carrying part of the worry set. Walk the pentagon and every tense a worry can occupy is checked exactly once.

**Distinguisher and counter per card.** The Distinguisher slot names the closest confusable card; the counter is Leahy's own move from the Ten Ways to Defeat Your Worry set (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005).

| Card | Closest confusable | The cut | Counter move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **11 Fortune-telling** | Catastrophizing (card 1) | **Prediction vs magnitude.** Fortune-telling asserts *that* the event happens; catastrophizing rates *how bad* it would be and whether you could stand it. "I'll fail that exam" is fortune-telling; "it would be terrible if I failed" is catastrophizing (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). | Put a number on it — how likely, 0–100%? Would you bet money at those odds? Then break the prediction into smaller steps and price each one; Leahy's worked case drops an 85% worry to 20% by that decomposition alone (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). |
| **12 What-if cascade** | Fortune-telling (card 11) | **One prediction vs a dependent chain.** Fortune-telling makes a single claim you can price. The cascade generates a series where each negative depends on the previous one and no answer satisfies — "Yeah, but what if I get anxious and can't catch my breath?" The give-away is the next *what if* arriving right after a satisfying answer (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). | Price the chain as a whole rather than link by link — a conjunction of dependent negatives is far less likely than any single link, and Leahy calls most such chains "highly implausible" (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). Then convert to an action question: "what is the immediate issue for me to address?" |
| **13 Unfair comparison** | Mental filter (card 6) / disqualifying-positive (card 7) | **Filtered evidence vs filtered reference class.** Mental filter drops your own positive data. Unfair comparison keeps all your data and scores it against only the people who did better, against a *receding reference point* that moves as you approach it (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). | Restore the distribution: compare downward and against your own past, not only upward, and test whether the standard is one a reasonable person would apply (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). |
| **14 Regret orientation** | Should-statements (card 9) | **Tense of the rule.** Should-statements assert a rule about now or next; regret orientation runs the rule backwards, judging a past decision against information that only arrived afterwards. Leahy names the mechanism *hindsight bias* — believing after the fact that you knew before the fact (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). | Reconstruct the decision-time information set: what did you actually know then, and what would a reasonable person holding only that have done? Leahy's split is between "I must be an idiot for not checking everything" and "I did what any reasonable person would have done" (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). |
| **15 Inability to disconfirm** | Disqualifying-positive (card 7) | **Local vs structural.** Disqualifying-positive dismisses one specific piece of good evidence by category. Inability to disconfirm is a property of the *belief*: it is stated so that no possible observation could count against it — "my friends hang out with me only because they feel sorry for me" absorbs the friends as evidence *for* the worry (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). | Ask the falsifier before the counter: *what would count as evidence I am wrong?* If there is no answer, the thought is not a prediction and no counter will bind it — route it (see Routing Boundary below). Note also that avoidance is why the disconfirming data never arrives: dodging the situation removes the chance to find out you could handle it (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005) — the mechanism owned by worry-as-emotional-avoidance. |

**Contradiction to flag.** The deck's original Distinguisher line above says "Catastrophizing predicts events". Leahy's Table 6.3 does not: it assigns event-prediction to fortune-telling and defines catastrophizing purely as the unbearability rating — "so awful and unbearable that you won't be able to stand it" (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). Both usages exist in the CBT literature. This page leaves the older line standing and adopts Leahy's narrower split for cards 11–15; in practice, whenever card 11 is on the table, read card 1 as the **magnitude** card. Unresolved — see Open Questions.

**Merged, not added.** Leahy's Table 6.3 lists seventeen distortions. Ten are the deck's existing cards under other names, so they were merged rather than duplicated (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005):

| Leahy's name | Existing card |
|---|---|
| Mind reading | Mind-reading (2) |
| Catastrophizing | Catastrophizing (1) — narrowed, see the contradiction note |
| Labeling | Labeling (10) |
| Discounting positives | Disqualifying-positive (7) |
| Negative filter | Mental filter (6) |
| Overgeneralizing | Over-generalization (5) |
| All-or-nothing thinking | All-or-nothing (4) |
| Shoulds | Should-statements (9) |
| Personalizing | Personalization (3) |
| Emotional reasoning | Emotional reasoning (8) |

Leahy's remaining two — *Blaming* and *Judgment focus* — were **not** carded. They are the other-directed mirrors of Personalization and Labeling (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005), which is the same self-vs-other split the Open Questions already raise for Should-statements; minting three half-cards for one unresolved question would inflate the confusion set without adding a cut. Held there instead.

## SPEAR Thought-Record

| Slot | Content |
|---|---|
| **Scene** | Trigger event — one sentence: *what happened, then a thought appeared* |
| **Preconditions** | Current state on [PULSE](./pulse-overview.md) axes; evidence at hand (what you actually know, separate from what you feel) |
| **Execution** | **Name** the distortion from the deck → **Write** the counter (re-statement that respects the evidence, not toxic positivity) → **Log** the entry |
| **Alternatives** | A second possible reading of the event consistent with the evidence (not necessarily nicer — just *also possible*) |
| **Repair** | At 24h, re-read the entry — did the counter hold? Did a new distortion appear? |

The counter is anchored on **evidence**, not on hope. A bad counter ("everything will be fine") is worse than no counter (it teaches the operator their tool doesn't work). A good counter explicitly cites what you *do* know, separate from what you feel.

## What Counts as a Counter (vs. Suppression vs. Toxic Positivity)

| Type | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Counter (good)** | Names the felt fear, cites evidence, narrows the claim | "I'm scared the review will be bad. Evidence: the last 3 reviews on this code path were neutral or positive. The narrow claim is: this *specific reviewer* hasn't seen my code before, so uncertainty is real but bounded." |
| **Suppression (bad)** | Tells the feeling to go away | "Stop worrying. Just don't think about it." |
| **Toxic positivity (worse)** | Asserts a feel-good claim unanchored to evidence | "It's going to be amazing!" |

The give-away: a good counter could survive a hostile cross-examination. The other two cannot.

## Routing Boundary — a Catch Is Not a Container

Stated once, plainly, because it is the most common way this page gets misused:

**A distortion catch fixes one specific mis-thought. It does not bound an unbounded loop.**

The deck operates on a thought that has a *content* you can test — a prediction with a probability, a comparison with a reference class, a decision with an information set. Name it, counter it, done. That is a bounded unit of work, and the SPEAR timebox above is sized for it.

An unbounded catastrophizing or what-if loop is a different object. It has no fixed content to test: each counter you write is answered by a fresh *what if*, so the catch simply becomes fuel. Countering harder makes the loop longer, not shorter. The tells are card 12 (the cascade re-spawning after a satisfying answer) and card 15 (no observation would end it) firing together, and the SPEAR timebox blowing past ten minutes with no counter written.

When that happens, **stop countering and change containers**: the loop routes to worry time, which owns the productive/unproductive split and the scheduled-container move. Two neighbouring routes complete the boundary — if the loop is really a demand for certainty, it is intolerance-of-uncertainty's object (that page owns IU and safety behaviours); if the worry is doing a job for you emotionally, it is worry-as-emotional-avoidance's.

This is the counterpart of the existing state route: if the problem is **state**, go to resilience-and-recovery-os; if the problem is **unboundedness**, go to worry time; only if the problem is a **specific testable thought** does the deck apply.

## Drill Ladder

| Stage | Drill | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|
| **Lamp** | Read 15 short thought-fragments — label the distortion from the 15-card deck | ≥12/15 in ≤8s/fragment |
| **Scale** | For each labeled fragment, write a 1-sentence evidence-anchored counter | All 15 pass the cross-examination test (no toxic positivity, no suppression) |
| **Sword** | Catch a *live* thought during a real session — name it within 60s of appearance, write counter within 5 min | ≥3 caught/week, sustained for 4 weeks |
| **Reflex** | Promote your top-3 personal distortions to [reflex labels](./automaticity-and-reflex-training.md) — recognition runs in ≤10s | Cold-test: post-event re-read still flags the same 3 as primary |

Most operators have 2–3 distortions that account for ~80% of their bad thoughts. Reflex-promotion is targeted at those, not the whole deck.

**Where cards 11–15 enter.** They enter at **Lamp**, on the existing ladder and the existing floors — no new scale (this page's Lamp · Scale · Sword · Reflex stages sit on the drill-ladder axis owned by [skill-progression-stages](./skill-progression-stages.md)). Growing the deck from ten cards to fifteen changes the *confusion set*, not the pass criterion: still ≥12/15 fragments at ≤8s/fragment for Lamp, still ≤10s recognition for a reflex-promoted card. The honest expectation is a temporary Lamp-score dip while the four new distinguisher pairs (11 vs 1, 12 vs 11, 13 vs 6/7, 15 vs 7) settle. If Lamp has not returned to floor after two weeks of drilling, the fault is the distinguishers, not the operator — sharpen the cut, do not lower the floor.

## Measurement (METER)

`thought_record_event`:

```
{
  "trigger": "<one sentence>",
  "distortion_primary": "catastrophizing | mind_reading | ... | fortune_telling | what_if_cascade | unfair_comparison | regret_orientation | inability_to_disconfirm",
  "distortion_secondary": [...],
  "routed_out": null | "worry_time" | "recovery" | "decision",
  "time_to_label_sec": <int>,
  "counter_quality": "evidence_anchored | suppression | toxic_positivity | unscored",
  "counter_held_at_24h": true | false | "tbd",
  "state_at_trigger": { "E": 1-5, "S": 1-5 },
  "state_after_counter": { "E": 1-5, "S": 1-5 }
}
```

Weekly review reads:

1. **Distortion distribution** — top-3 stable? new distortion emerging?
2. **Time-to-label trend** — coming down? (the recognition reflex sharpening)
3. **24h-hold rate** — counter survives a day? (catches toxic positivity, which doesn't hold)
4. **State delta** — counter actually moved state, not just relabeled it
5. **Route rate** — share of entries with `routed_out` set. A rising worry-time share is *correct triage*, not failure (the boundary above is working); a rising *recovery* share means state is the real constraint.

Pass-floor: time-to-label trending down across 8 weeks; 24h-hold rate ≥60% (lower than recovery's because some counters get superseded by new information, which is healthy). Cards 11–15 add no new floor. One clarification they force: routed-out entries are **excluded from the 24h-hold denominator** — an unbounded loop that was correctly handed to worry time has no counter to hold, and scoring it as a miss would punish the right call.

## Failure Modes

- **Rumination disguised as analysis** — running the same trigger through 5 distortion lenses for an hour produces no counter. Counter: timebox the SPEAR to ≤10 minutes; if it doesn't resolve, the issue is upstream (state, not thought) — route to resilience-and-recovery-os.
- **Counter as suppression** — *"stop thinking that"* dressed as a thought-record. Catches: counter doesn't cite evidence; state delta is zero or negative. Counter: rewrite using the cross-examination test.
- **Toxic positivity** — the counter is feel-good but unfounded. Catches: 24h-hold is low; the next-day re-read makes the counter look naive. Counter: counters must include the *acknowledged risk* alongside the evidence.
- **Over-pathologizing normal feeling** — labeling ordinary disappointment as catastrophizing; labeling reasonable caution as mental-filter. Counter: a distortion is only a distortion if the *evidence does not support* the thought; if evidence supports it, the thought isn't distorted — the problem is just the *reality*, which is a different page.
- **Distortion shopping** — reaching for the favorite distortion (yours, not the real one). Counter: weekly distribution review; if 80% of your entries are one distortion, the deck isn't being applied — you're matching all triggers to your familiar label.
- **No-state-check** — running thought-records at S=5 produces low-quality counters. Counter: if S≥4, run recovery first; thought-mastery operates best at S≤3.
- **Countering a cascade** — writing counters at a card-12 what-if chain. Each counter is answered by a fresh *what if*, so the thought-record becomes the loop's fuel and the operator concludes the tool is broken (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). Catches: three or more counters written for one trigger; `routed_out` never set. Counter: apply the Routing Boundary — hand it to worry time via productive-vs-unproductive-worry instead of countering a fourth time.
- **Countering an unfalsifiable belief** — running the deck at a card-15 thought that no observation could contradict. The counter looks fine on the page and holds for zero days. Catch: the falsifier question ("what would count as evidence I'm wrong?") has no answer. Counter: the move is exposure to the avoided situation so disconfirming evidence can actually arrive (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005), not a better sentence.

## Integration With Neural OS

- **Parent (monitoring)** — metacognition is the awareness layer; this page is the intervention layer above it.
- **Adjacent (state)** — [PULSE](./pulse-overview.md) state read is a *precondition* — high-S thought-records produce bad counters; route to recovery first.
- **Adjacent (impulse)** — emotional-reasoning + escape urges often co-fire; the named-distortion catch from this page and the named-urge catch from delayed-gratification-gym are the same shape and can run as one cycle.
- **Adjacent (decisions)** — when a thought-record surfaces that the *decision* (not the thought) is the issue, hand off to [decision-kernel](./decision-kernel.md) for the values/reversibility pass.
- **Adjacent (re-frame)** — when the distortion turns out to be "I'm running the wrong frame because the context shifted," route to adaptability-os.
- **Downstream (resilience)** — the cognitive-channel recovery move in resilience-and-recovery-os is *this page's* SPEAR procedure applied during collapse.
- **Sibling (belief layer)** — [ants-and-lies-of-learning](./ants-and-lies-of-learning.md) owns the ANT/LIE framing and links here for the taxonomy. When a well-written counter keeps failing at 24h, the fault is usually a belief underneath the thought — that is its layer, not this one.
- **Boundary (worry)** — productive-vs-unproductive-worry takes the unbounded loops this deck cannot bound; intolerance-of-uncertainty takes the certainty demands underneath them; worry-as-emotional-avoidance explains why the loop is being *maintained* rather than dropped. leahy-worry-cure is the seven-step protocol all three sit inside, and the origin of cards 11–15.
- **Boundary (somatic)** — health-anxiety runs the same catch-name-counter cycle on a *behaviour* (body-scanning/checking) rather than a thought; its "prove a negative" engine is card 15 (inability to disconfirm) applied to bodily sensation.

## Open Questions

- Is "should-statements" actually two cards (self-directed vs other-directed)? Different counters; same surface.
- Should the 15-card deck be augmented with positive-thought distortions (over-confidence, planning fallacy)? Currently the deck is biased toward negative thoughts; high-energy errors are under-covered.
- Empirical: does the cross-examination test (could the counter survive interrogation?) generalize, or is it specific to logically-styled operators?
- Card 1 is now ambiguous between two literatures — event-prediction (the original line on this page) and unbearability-rating (Leahy's Table 6.3, source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). Should card 1 be rewritten as pure magnitude now that card 11 carries prediction, or does that break the older entries logged against it?
- The self-vs-other split keeps recurring: Should-statements (self/other), Personalization vs Leahy's *Blaming*, Labeling vs Leahy's *Judgment focus* (source: Leahy, The Worry Cure, 2005). Is this one axis that should be a **field** on every card (`direction: self | other`) rather than three card-splits? A field would keep the deck at 15 and still capture the counter difference.
- Do cards 11–15 pull their weight outside worry? Fortune-telling and unfair comparison plainly generalize; regret orientation and inability-to-disconfirm may be worry-specific enough to warrant a sub-deck flag rather than full deck membership.

2. Distortion → predict counter
3. No answer to "what would prove me wrong" → predict the counter fails at 24h

2. Recurring → drill counter to reflex
3. Cascade or unfalsifiable → hand to worry time, log `routed_out`
