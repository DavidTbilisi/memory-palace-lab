# Five Attributes of Strategic Thinking

**Summary**: The five **universal attributes** Sloan's executive interviews surfaced as essential to learning to think strategically — irrespective of socio-national culture. **Imagination · Broad perspective · Juggle · No-control-over · Desire-to-win.** They are universal, learnable, and in **simultaneous interplay** (no fixed sequence). Critical reflective processes strengthen them, and the arts develop them. They are both the *affliction* and the *antidote* — the tension between them is the productive substrate for strategic thinking.

**Sources**:
- [sloan-learning-to-think-strategically](./sloan-learning-to-think-strategically.md) — source summary

## The five attributes

| # | Attribute | One-line | Anchor quote |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Imagination** | Vivid; sees beyond the data; engages a-rational thinking | "Your mind and imagination have to kick in if you're going to be any good." |
| 2 | **Broad perspective** | Panoramic + diverse-frame view; bird's-eye | "If you want to be competitive, you must find a way to take a step back to go to the highest point … then look at what happens below." |
| 3 | **Juggle** | Handle incomplete + inconsistent + contradictory information simultaneously | "Learn to juggle! [You're] bombarded with tons of information … and it changes all the time." |
| 4 | **No control over** | Pay attention to what you *cannot* control rather than what you can | "You'd better spend it thinking about what you can't control." |
| 5 | **Desire to win** | Relentless, fierce, must-win attitude | "If you don't have that drive, you're not the one to make strategy — it won't be strong or good enough." |

The attributes are **not ranked**. Sloan: *they are all equally essential to learning to think strategically*.

## Each attribute, expanded

### 1. Imagination

Vivid; creative; generates unforeseen possibilities. Not the same as creativity (which combines unrelated dimensions); imagination is the *capacity* to see what is not literally present.

- **Develops through**: arts engagement (painting, photography, music, drama, novels), travel, exposure to unfamiliar contexts.
- **Common failure**: dismissed as "not serious" in business contexts; relegated to a "vision" exercise once a year. Sloan: the dismissal is itself the failure.
- **Cross-link**: [strategic-thinking-cognitive-cluster](./strategic-thinking-cognitive-cluster.md) §Creative + Generative + Symbolic.

### 2. Broad perspective

Two parts:
- **Panoramic view** — literally see the strategic problem from a bird's-eye perspective; zoom out from the immediate details
- **Diverse frames** — draw on many different points of view; include outsiders, contrarians, non-experts

The hot-air-balloon-pilot executive Sloan quotes uses *exactly* this metaphor:

> When I compete in a hot air balloon race, I try to find the wind blowing in the right direction. I position my balloon at the highest point above the other balloons … and then I make a decision … I apply this same strategy to my business.

- **Develops through**: living and working outside your home country; talking to vastly diverse people; participating in multiple unrelated communities.
- **Common failure**: corporate culture's same-frame thinking (cf. [Conformists trap](./six-framing-traps.md)); the diverse-frame half collapses.
- **Cross-link**: [strategic-thinking-cognitive-cluster](./strategic-thinking-cognitive-cluster.md) §Panoramic; [seven-strategic-thinking-dimensions](./seven-strategic-thinking-dimensions.md) (the diverse-frame discipline structured).

### 3. Juggle

Hold many incomplete, inconsistent, contradictory pieces of information simultaneously *without collapsing to a simplified single view*. The strategic-thinking counterpoint to the strategic-planning move of prioritization/reduction.

Sloan, citing Kahneman:

> Like a juggler with several balls in the air, you cannot afford to slow down; the rate at which material decays in memory forces the pace, driving you to refresh and rehearse information before it is lost.

This attribute is **the working-memory load** of strategic thinking. Maps onto [working-memory](./working-memory.md) in the wiki — strategists with higher working-memory capacity have a structural advantage at the juggle attribute. Drills that expand working memory (Soroban substrate, peg-matrix anchoring) are upstream of juggle development.

- **Develops through**: working with deliberately ambiguous data; multi-context responsibilities; explicit drilling.
- **Common failure**: the over-simplifier trap (cf. [six-framing-traps](./six-framing-traps.md) #6).
- **Cross-link**: [and-polarity-thinking](./and-polarity-thinking.md) (the AND polarity is a 2-ball juggle minimum); [working-memory](./working-memory.md) (substrate).

### 4. No control over

The counter-intuitive attribute: **pay attention to what you cannot control, not what you can**. Sloan's research was emphatic — successful strategists obsess over the uncontrollable, not the controllable, because:

- The uncontrollable is where the surprises live (and where competitors can't reach either)
- Attention to the controllable produces operational excellence, not strategic edge
- The competitive advantage comes from reading the uncontrollable correctly

Sloan: *successful strategists viewed their lack of control as a kind of tool for leveraging risk*.

- **Develops through**: working in environments with high uncontrolled-variable density (foreign countries, volatile markets, hobbies with material limits — the ceramicist who cannot control clay properties);
- **Common failure**: the [Immediate Gratifier trap](./six-framing-traps.md) — control-craving manifests as premature action over the controllable;
- **Cross-link**: [PULSE](./pulse-overview.md) (the controllable inputs the strategist *can* govern — Energy, Stress — leaving the uncontrollable for strategic attention).

### 5. Desire to win

A fierce, relentless, **must-win** attitude. The affective driver behind every strategy. Without it, the strategist will not endure the discomfort of the other four attributes:

- Imagination requires tolerating the discomfort of "I don't know what this is yet"
- Broad perspective requires tolerating contradictory inputs
- Juggle requires tolerating ambiguity
- No-control-over requires tolerating helplessness

The desire to win is what makes those discomforts tolerable. It is the **affective fuel**.

- **Develops through**: identifying what you genuinely care about (often non-work); building competitive practice in that domain; importing the must-win discipline.
- **Common failure**: faux-desire (saying you want to win while protecting an exit); diluted desire (caring about too many things at once); externalized desire (winning only when someone else is watching).
- **Cross-link**: [failure-mechanism](./failure-mechanism.md) (the identity-layer fuel); [growth-mindset](./growth-mindset.md) (the belief gate above the fuel).

## The interplay — the attributes as a system

Sloan: the attributes operate in **simultaneous interplay**, not sequence. A typical strategic-thinking move uses several at once:

- **Imagining what could be → Broad perspective on consequences → Juggling the new variables it introduces → Acknowledging what can't be controlled in the new arrangement → Driven through the discomfort by the desire to win**

The interplay is **paradoxical**: each attribute generates tension with the others (imagination produces *more* things to juggle; broad perspective surfaces *more* uncontrollables; juggle tires the desire to win). And yet — per Sloan — the attributes are also the *antidote* to the tension:

> The five attributes are both the affliction and the antidote.

This is itself an [AND polarity](./and-polarity-thinking.md) at the personal-attribute layer.

## Self-assessment

Rate yourself 0-5 on each attribute (Sloan does not provide a formal instrument; this is the wiki's wrapper):

- **Imagination**: When was the last time you generated a novel scenario nobody else in the room had?
- **Broad perspective**: How many distinct frames did you import into your last strategy conversation?
- **Juggle**: How many contradictory data points did you hold without collapsing to a single view in your last hard problem?
- **No control over**: What % of your last strategy session was spent on what you can vs cannot control?
- **Desire to win**: When you imagine losing this strategy, what does it feel like? Mild disappointment, or unbearable?

**Floor**: 3 on weakest. **Pass**: 4 on at least two; 3+ on all five.

## Failure modes

1. **Attribute-cherry-picking** — developing only the attributes you're already good at. Defense: explicit weakest-attribute focus per quarter.
2. **Faux desire-to-win** — claiming the drive without the lived experience of losing in the past. Defense: name what you've lost that hurt enough to remember; if nothing, the desire is not yet earned.
3. **Imagination without grounding** — imagination unchecked by juggle produces fantasy. Defense: pair imagination work with data juggle work in the same session.
4. **Broad perspective as procrastination** — adding frames forever instead of acting. Defense: time-box the frame-adding phase.
5. **No-control fatalism** — attention to uncontrollables collapses into helplessness, not strategic attention. Defense: pair with desire-to-win.

## Mnemonic

A **Velvet Aeon Cosmic-mode figure** stands at the center of a stone circle with **five great pillars**, each engraved with a glyph. The figure wears **five sashes** of different colors, each tied to a pillar by a slender silver chain:

- **Imagination — gold sash**, tied to a pillar with a **stylized eye open inside a flame**
- **Broad perspective — silver sash**, tied to a pillar with a **bird's silhouette against a panoramic mountain ring**
- **Juggle — green sash**, tied to a pillar with **seven small spinning orbs in mid-arc** (corresponding to the [7 strategic dimensions](./seven-strategic-thinking-dimensions.md))
- **No control over — violet sash**, tied to a pillar with **a hand releasing a feather to the wind, palm facing up**
- **Desire to win — crimson sash**, tied to a pillar with **a single sharp arrow embedded deep in the pillar's stone**, head buried, shaft humming

The figure's face is **strong** (per feedback_image_face_and_hair, because the must-win attitude requires power) with long hair lifted in the circle's wind. The five chains form a **five-pointed inward star**, each chain humming at its own tension. The figure cannot move freely — moving toward any pillar tightens the chains to the other four. The figure stands still in the center, **head tilted slightly upward** — strategic thinking is the act of *not* moving toward any single pillar.
