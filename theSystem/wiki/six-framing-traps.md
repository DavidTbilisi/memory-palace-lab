# Six Framing Traps

**Summary**: Sloan's named taxonomy of **six personal anti-patterns** that impede reframing — habits of mind that look like strengths in some contexts but kill strategic thinking. Each trap is *useful* somewhere (an expert mindset has value; conformity bonds teams); each becomes a *trap* when the situation demands reframing and the habit refuses. The taxonomy sits at the **personality + role layer** above [anti-tactic-detection](./anti-tactic-detection.md) (which sits at the puzzle / problem layer) and complements mbti-shadow-and-stress (the function-stack stress version of the same dynamic).

**Sources**:
- [sloan-learning-to-think-strategically](./sloan-learning-to-think-strategically.md) — source summary
- Sloan, *Learning to Think Strategically* (4th ed., 2020), Ch 8 §"Six framing traps that impede reframing"

## The six traps

| # | Trap | The strength it warps | When it kills strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Immediate Gratifiers** | Bias to action; fast execution | When the situation needs Thinking (suspend solutions); produces premature plans |
| 2 | **Know-it-alls** | Confidence; decisiveness | When the situation needs new frames; the trap freezes the existing frame and stops dialogue |
| 3 | **Experts** | Pattern recognition; speed | When the situation has *novel* elements the expert mistakes for familiar ones (cf. Tetlock's "experts with the most knowledge are often less reliable") |
| 4 | **Satisfieds** | Stability; team cohesion | When the situation requires disrupting the comfortable status quo; produces resistance dressed as "we already know what works" |
| 5 | **Conformists** | Group bonding; ritual; loyalty | When the situation needs the contrarian; conformists make same-frame thinking feel virtuous |
| 6 | **Over-simplifiers** | Clarity; communication | When the problem is irreducibly complex; the simplification *removes* the strategic substance |

## Detailed descriptions

### 1. Immediate Gratifiers

> These are the people who want results — now! They are all about action, with no time, experience, or appreciation for the thinking that underlies and improves strategic action. Once we begin to lose our ability to imagine or create, we cannot envision the future, and strategic thinking capacity vanishes.

**Where the strength lives**: emergency response, operational tempo, sales close-out. **Where the trap fires**: any strategic-thinking session that requires *not* deciding yet.

**Counter-protocol**: explicit time-boxing of the no-decision phase ("we will not pick during this meeting"). State the rule before the meeting starts.

### 2. Know-it-alls

> Know-it-alls tend to make instant judgments based on frozen, same-frame thinking. They believe their frame because it is the only way they "know." Challenge and testing feel wrong and threatening. Know-it-all thinking stops dialogue, freezes us in place, and leads us to ignore information and insights that support other positions.

**Where the strength lives**: domain expertise + clear instructions to a junior team. **Where the trap fires**: any situation requiring [critical dialogue](./critical-dialogue-discussion-debate.md) or [premise reflection](./content-process-premise-reflection.md) — both stop in the presence of certainty.

**Counter-protocol**: explicit role rotation (the know-it-all is asked to *only* ask questions for one meeting). Pair with [nine-dive-question-drill](./nine-dive-question-drill.md) discipline.

### 3. Experts

> An expert mindset can be a double-edged sword. Sometimes, highly expert people make up their minds so quickly that they overlook critical flaws in the process because of their deep knowledge, successful experience, and acquired and trusted intuition. Expert minds are like computers that can scan vast amounts of data with few interruptions. Because of their vast experience and familiarity, experts can fail to consider surprise elements which may be crucial factors in their strategic problem. Those who lack specific expertise and think more slowly can have an unexpected advantage.

The wiki's existing [ok-plateau](./ok-plateau.md) page is closely related: expertise that Coagulates the *crux* is exactly the Sloan expert-trap firing.

**Where the strength lives**: pattern-rich, recurring problems with stable structure. **Where the trap fires**: novel problems that look like familiar ones (the *false familiarity* trap — see also Tetlock, *Expert Political Judgment*).

**Counter-protocol**: invite the non-expert into the framing session; the non-expert's slower processing surfaces the cues the expert auto-filters. Pair with explicit [anti-tactic scan](./anti-tactic-detection.md) ("am I being primed to see this as the familiar case?").

### 4. Satisfieds

> These people are satisfied and content with the status quo and see no reason to change. This kind of resistance to strategic thinking is based on a rationale that if we want to preserve our privileged place in a particular corporate niche — the one that lets me in regardless of whom it shut out — then critical dialogue, critical inquiry or critical reflection is a threat to our sense of self and our position. Satisfieds sometimes need to be reminded that discomfort, imbalance, and discontent are powerful motivators for learning and necessary for strategic thinking.

**Where the strength lives**: maintenance phase of a stable business; preserving institutional knowledge. **Where the trap fires**: any situation in which the team's privileged place is the *cause* of the strategic problem.

**Counter-protocol**: name the privilege explicitly. Ask: "If you weren't already in this position, would you defend it?" Pair with the [memory-paradox](./memory-paradox.md) discipline (take seriously, hold lightly).

### 5. Conformists

> Similar to the satisfieds, conformists tend to have a club sort of mentality. Once we decide that our group is superior, our mind focuses only on who is one of us and who is not — and what frames, actions, objects, or thoughts maintain that distinction. Symbolism overrides thinking. … But when challenge is required, the same-frame thinking of conformists makes the group's ability to detect the signals of change and to envision a new thinking impossible.

**Where the strength lives**: team identity, ritual, brand consistency. **Where the trap fires**: any situation in which the in-group's frame is the wrong frame.

**Counter-protocol**: import outsider voices into strategy sessions (per Sloan's "Vary the usual composition of strategy groups regularly"). Name conformity when it appears; reward contrarian questions.

### 6. Over-simplifiers

> As situations become more complex and contradictory, a common tendency when making sense is to over-simplify. Because we feel overwhelmed, we sometimes falsely believe that all complex and contradictory things can be reduced to more basic parts for the sake of expedience and relief of tension. We deny the actual complexity because we do not know how to make meaning of competing and contradictory elements that are inherent in strategic problems. The temptation for over-simplification in a chaotic and fast-paced environment is tremendous, and we often become vulnerable to shortcuts and quick fixes or forcing complex problems into a familiar frame as a means to expedience.

The wiki's existing [and-polarity-thinking](./and-polarity-thinking.md) page is the direct antidote: over-simplifiers collapse AND polarities to either/or. **Every collapsed polarity is an over-simplifier trap firing.**

**Where the strength lives**: communication to non-experts; quick decisions on routine matters. **Where the trap fires**: in the presence of an unresolved AND polarity; in the presence of irreducible complexity.

**Counter-protocol**: [AND polarity discipline](./and-polarity-thinking.md) + the [metaphor + drawing trio](./and-polarity-thinking.md#metaphor--drawing--and-polarity-trio). Force the trio whenever you suspect over-simplification.

## Cross-pattern observations

- **Traps 2, 3, 4, 5 are all forms of frame-protection** — defending the existing frame instead of examining it. They produce the *same-frame thinking* failure but from different motivations (certainty / expertise / privilege / belonging).
- **Traps 1 and 6 are forms of premature closure** — closing the cognitive process before the [premise](./content-process-premise-reflection.md) layer is reached. Different from frame-protection (which knows the frame and defends it) — these traps end the inquiry before the frame is even named.
- **The expert trap is the hardest to detect from inside**, because expertise is real and the pattern-match is *usually* correct. The "false familiarity" failure mode is rare per incident but high-impact per failure.
- **Each trap has a healthy form** at lower stakes; the trap fires when the stakes shift to *strategic* and the habit doesn't.

## Cross-link to MBTI typology

The 6 traps map onto MBTI function-stack patterns (see mbti-shadow-and-stress and mbti-temperaments-keirsey):

| Trap | Typological tendency |
|---|---|
| Immediate Gratifiers | SP (Artisan) under stress; Te-dom rushed |
| Know-it-alls | NT (Rational) Ti/Te overconfidence; especially INTJ / ENTJ inferior-function exposure |
| Experts | NT + experienced SJ; the Si-rich types pattern-match harder |
| Satisfieds | SJ (Guardian) — preserve-the-known is the temperament's core |
| Conformists | SJ (Guardian) + NF (Idealist) — belonging > truth when stressed |
| Over-simplifiers | NT seeking parsimony; ST seeking action; both can simplify wrongly |

The cross-link is *not* deterministic — anyone can fall into any trap. The mapping flags **which traps a given type's default cognitive habits make more likely**, useful as a self-diagnostic.

## METER

- `framing_trap_flag` (one of 6, multi-valued) — per-session diagnostic; pass <2 traps detected per session
- `trap_named_aloud` — boolean; did anyone explicitly name a trap firing? Pass ≥1/session
- `trap_self_named` vs `trap_other_named` — self-naming is the harder skill; track separately
- `over_simplifier_polarity_collapse` — special case: count of AND polarities collapsed within a session; pass <30%

## Failure modes (of the traps themselves)

1. **Trap-shaming** — naming someone's trap to attack them rather than the trap. Defense: name the trap as a *pattern*, not as a person ("the Know-it-all pattern is firing", not "you're being a know-it-all").
2. **Trap-hunting** — running the 6-trap scan so aggressively that every meeting feels like a trial. Defense: scan ~1 per meeting, not every minute.
3. **Trap-as-identity** — labeling yourself as "an Expert" and using it as permission to keep doing it. Defense: treat traps as states the person enters and exits, not stable identities.

## Mnemonic

A Velvet Aeon Cosmic-mode **stone chamber** with six recesses carved into the wall. In each recess stands a small statue representing one of the six traps:

1. **Immediate Gratifier** — a small bronze figure mid-stride, mouth open mid-shout, **a hammer raised but no anvil in sight** (action without target)
2. **Know-it-all** — a tall stone figure with **arms crossed**, **eyes closed**, **a sealed scroll clutched** (the answer is the closed scroll; no question opens it)
3. **Expert** — a glass figure with **finely articulated hands** but **a single feather growing through one palm** unnoticed (the cue the expert missed)
4. **Satisfied** — a velvet-clad figure **seated on a low cushion**, **smiling**, **a small chain anchoring the cushion to the wall** (the comfort is a tether)
5. **Conformist** — six identical small figures in a tight ring, all facing inward, **none facing the chamber door** (the in-group has its back to the world)
6. **Over-simplifier** — a figure holding a **complex tangle of colored threads in the left hand** and a **single straight thread in the right**, having **cut the rest** (simplification by amputation)

In the chamber's center, a **luminous woman** (fragile/luminous face per feedback_image_face_and_hair) walks a slow circle around the six statues, holding a **small lantern**. Wherever her lantern touches a statue, the statue **softens momentarily** — the lantern is the [nine-dive-question-drill](./nine-dive-question-drill.md) firing. The chamber's single light source comes from above; no atmospheric noise; the sky outside is empty.
