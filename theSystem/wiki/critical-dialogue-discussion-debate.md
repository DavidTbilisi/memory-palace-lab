# Critical Dialogue · Discussion · Debate

**Summary**: Sloan's three named communicative techniques in strategic thinking — each with a distinct *intent*, *direction*, and *role*. **Dialogue** explores and searches (divergent, nonlinear, partner-as-equal); **discussion** establishes and fixes (convergent, linear, partner-as-equal-to-convince); **debate** defends and argues (convergent, linear, partner-as-rival). The three are **complementary**, not ranked — strategic thinking uses *all three* at different moments. The load-bearing move is explicit signaling: state which technique you are using *before* the meeting starts.

**Sources**:
- [sloan-learning-to-think-strategically](./sloan-learning-to-think-strategically.md) — source summary
- Sloan, *Learning to Think Strategically* (4th ed., 2020), Ch 10 §"Comparison: critical dialogue, discussion, and debate", Table 10.1

## The three techniques side-by-side

| Aspect | **Dialogue** | **Discussion** | **Debate** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Etymology | Greek *dia + logos* = "meaning flowing through" | Latin *discussus* = "investigate by reasoning or argument" | Latin *de + battuere* = "to beat" |
| Definition | To **explore and search** | To **establish or fix** | To **defend or argue** |
| Direction | **Divergent** (emerging proposition; nonlinear) | **Convergent** (push-pull proposition; linear) | **Convergent** (win-lose proposition; linear) |
| Aim | Create individual + collective meaning through expression and empathetic listening | Find a solution; investigate and examine; aim is single agreement or conclusion | Win by persuading opponent; convictions already held |
| Partner is | An equal to be **understood** | An equal to be **convinced** | A rival to be **defeated** |
| Posture | Suspend opinions · inquire and disclose | Present and convince in detail of a predetermined position · give answers and explanations | Judgmental · competitive · combative |
| Cognitive cluster | Divergent / a-rational (strategic-thinking cluster) | Convergent / rational (planning cluster) | Convergent / rational (planning cluster) |
| Learning domain | [Dive](./surf-dive-learning-domains.md) | Mostly [Surf](./surf-dive-learning-domains.md) | Mostly [Surf](./surf-dive-learning-domains.md) |

## Three factors of a good critical dialogue

A "good" strategy dialogue (per Sloan) has three properties:

1. **Informed** — substantive knowledge about some aspect of the topic; data, context, prior thinking. Springs from curiosity. Without it the dialogue degrades into ungrounded chatter.
2. **Risky** — challenges underlying assumptions and beliefs; touches raw nerves; demands vulnerability. The affective dimension is unmistakably present. Without it the dialogue degrades into diplomatic theater.
3. **Critical** — brings partners to challenge and divergence. Not for attacking (debate) or convincing (discussion). Reflecting and responding to questions becomes the central activity. Without it the dialogue collapses to discussion.

The three factors are themselves an [AND polarity triple](./and-polarity-thinking.md): informed AND risky AND critical, with no one absorbing the others.

## When to use which

| Phase of strategic work | Primary technique | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-strategy framing (no problem named yet) | **Dialogue** | Need divergence; no premature closure |
| Confirming what we know vs don't know | **Discussion** | Need establishment; data + claims, push-pull |
| Testing the strongest version of an opposing view | **Debate** | Need stress; the position must survive attack |
| Identifying the AND polarity | **Dialogue** | Polarities surface only in divergent conversation |
| Choosing among 3 candidate strategies | **Discussion** | Convergent, but not adversarial |
| Pressure-testing the chosen strategy | **Debate** | Adversarial stress-test is the right rigor |
| Post-mortem / [AAR](./after-action-review.md) | **Dialogue + Discussion** | First dialogue (what happened, why), then discussion (what next) |

The faucet metaphor Sloan quotes (global head of engineering, medical equipment company):

> If we want to expand our thinking and need a rush of ideas or divergent thinking, then we need to "open the faucet" and practice critical dialogue. On the other hand, if we have thoroughly explored ideas, have listened, tested opinions, invited contrary positions, and have a wide range of people involved, then we may need to gradually converge and "close the faucet" by defining a problem through discussion. In order to make a decision and draw conclusions, eventually, we may want to use debate to "turn off" the faucet and stop the flow.

**Open → Narrow → Close**. Dialogue opens, discussion narrows, debate closes.

## Explicit signaling — the discipline

The most common failure: starting a meeting without naming the technique, then sliding between all three in a single conversation while different participants think they're doing different things.

The discipline:
- **Before the meeting**: "Part A is dialogue; Part B is debate. Please come prepared for both."
- **At the meeting open**: re-state the technique. "We're in dialogue mode now. No conclusions today."
- **At transitions**: signal the switch. "We're closing the dialogue. Discussion next."
- **When someone violates the technique**: name it neutrally. "That was debate language — let's hold it for Part B."

## The 9-question dive drill is a dialogue tool

The [nine-dive-question-drill](./nine-dive-question-drill.md) (Why? Why not? Whose frame? Other frames? Missing frames? What else? Where else? When else? How else?) is specifically a **dialogue** technique — not discussion, not debate. The questions are exploratory, not conclusive. Using them in discussion mode produces nothing; using them in debate mode is read as attack.

## After-Action Review and other practices

- [AAR](./after-action-review.md): a structured **dialogue** with discussion phases at the end (Q5 "What do we do now?")
- **Coding of surf/dive questions** during meetings (Sloan recommends): tracks which technique the meeting actually used vs claimed
- **Outsider invitations**: dialogue benefits most from outsiders; debate benefits least (debate against an outsider can devolve to talking past each other)

- `dialogue_minutes` vs `discussion_minutes` vs `debate_minutes` per strategy session; pass dialogue ≥40% of total time for Thinking-dot sessions
- `technique_named_at_open` — boolean per meeting; pass ≥80% of strategy meetings
- `technique_violation_count` — incidents of debate language in dialogue mode; pass ≤2/session
- `9_dive_question_round_count` — drill firings per dialogue; pass ≥1/dialogue

## Failure modes

1. **Unnamed-technique meetings** — most common; the meeting drifts between all three with participants in different modes. Defense: explicit signaling.
2. **Dialogue-collapses-to-debate** — happens when challenge feels like attack; the dialogue is gone. Defense: name the violation; reset to dialogue posture.
3. **Discussion-pretending-to-be-dialogue** — the team is converging toward a predetermined position while calling the meeting a dialogue. Defense: the test is whether the convener is willing to be surprised; if not, it's discussion.
4. **Debate-without-prior-dialogue** — testing a strategy before exploring frames; produces narrow, defensible plans that miss the actual strategic problem. Defense: never debate until dialogue has produced multiple frames.
5. **Faux dialogue with the privileged** — junior voices not heard because of hierarchy; the technique is named but not lived. Defense: explicit role-rotation, outsider invitations.

## Mnemonic

A Velvet Aeon Cosmic-mode **circular hall** with three doors set into the wall, each carved with a glyph:

- **Dialogue door** — open archway with **billowing curtains**; through it, a circle of seated figures, each with a **small light cupped in their palms**, all leaning toward the center, **mouths open mid-question**; no chairs at the head of the circle; no podium. Above the door: ⟳ (the divergent flow glyph).
- **Discussion door** — a rectangular frame with a **set ledger** at its threshold; through it, two figures across a table, **sliding written claims back and forth**, one nodding, one counter-writing; the room narrows in perspective toward a single inked decision. Above the door: → (the convergent flow glyph).
- **Debate door** — an iron-bound portal; through it, **two armored figures facing across a stone arena**, each holding a **bronze shield etched with their conviction**, **a third figure (the judge) seated above**; weapons drawn but not striking. Above the door: ⚔ (the win-lose glyph).

A **luminous woman** stands at the hall's center holding **three small keys on a single ring** — she is the meeting convener. Above her, three lanterns dim and brighten according to which door the keys are currently used in. **Only one lantern is bright at a time** — the explicit-signaling discipline. Velvet Aeon sub-mode: **sacred memory** (the woman remembers which key opened which door in which meeting). Long hair lifted in a single breeze from the hall's open ceiling.
