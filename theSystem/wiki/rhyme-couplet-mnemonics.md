# Rhyme-Couplet Mnemonics

**Summary**: A verbal/auditory Tier-2 encoder primitive that compresses a **cue → action** pair (a rule, discipline, or trigger-response) into a short rhyming line you'd actually say out loud — e.g. *"Don't stack the deck — pick one, then check."* Rhyme + tight meter + brevity make the line stick in the phonological loop and fire fast as a reflex cue. It rides **inside** the Tier-1 encoders ([NEDF](./nedf-overview.md) name-hook, [SPEAR](./spear-overview.md) cue, [automaticity](./automaticity-and-reflex-training.md) trigger), not as a standalone system. Cataloged in [mnemonic-methods-master](./mnemonic-methods-master.md).

## What it is

A **rhyme-couplet** is a one-line (or two-line) mnemonic that pairs a **cue** with an **action or payoff** and binds them with rhyme and meter so the whole pair recalls as a single sound-object. It is the verbal-rhythm sibling of the [Morse](./symbolic-encoding-systems.md) rhythm encoder: where Morse encodes *exact letters* as rhythm, a rhyme-couplet encodes a *rule or trigger* as rhythm-plus-meaning.

It is **not** for concepts, graphs, procedures, or exact numbers — those have dedicated encoders. It is for the thin, high-frequency layer of material that is best remembered as a *saying*: disciplines, trigger-action rules, watch-fors, one-line protocols.

## Why it works (one mechanism)

Three forces stack:

1. **Rhyme constrains recall** — once you remember the first half, the rhyme word narrows the second half to almost one option. The couplet self-checks.
2. **Meter rides the phonological loop** — a tight, regular beat is rehearsed and replayed more cheaply than prose.
3. **Processing fluency reads as truth and salience** — fluent, rhyming lines are judged more memorable and more credible (the "rhyme-as-reason" effect), which is exactly what you want for a rule you must *trust and act on under load*.

The payoff is **speed at the cue point**: a regulation move or discipline you can say in one breath fires faster than a paragraph you must reconstruct.

## Best for

| Use it for | Don't use it for |
|---|---|
| Trigger → action rules ("when X, do Y") | Concepts / definitions → [NEDF](./nedf-overview.md) |
| Disciplines & one-line protocols | Multi-step procedures → [SPEAR](./spear-overview.md) |
| Watch-fors / failure-mode reminders | Relationships between concepts → [CAST](./cast-overview.md) |
| Reflex cues for [automatic trigger-actions](./automaticity-and-reflex-training.md) | Exact numbers → PAO + Major System |
| Short menus held as a "deck" of lines | Exact spelling → [Braille / Morse](./symbolic-encoding-systems.md) |

## Construction recipe

Five steps, ~30 seconds per line:

1. **Reduce to a core** — boil the move to 2–5 words: the cue and the payoff. ("pick one move" + "then re-check.")
2. **Find the rhyme** — rhyme or near-rhyme the payoff word. ("check / deck"; "press / mess"; "out / doubt.")
3. **Cast the line** — write it as a couplet *or* a single internal-rhyme line; keep each side ~4–8 syllables so the beat stays tight.
4. **Lead with a verb** — prefer the imperative ("pick," "press," "dim," "rock"); a rule should *command*, not describe.
5. **Say it out loud** — if you'd actually speak it, it stays; if it feels like a tongue-twister or a stretch, cut it. (This is the checksum.)

## Riding inside the Tier-1 encoders

Per [mnemonic-methods-master](./mnemonic-methods-master.md), a Tier-2 primitive feeds a Tier-1 card; it does not replace it:

- **NEDF name-hook** — use a couplet as the Name-hook so the concept's retrieval handle is a saying.
- **SPEAR cue / HEART watch-for** — the trigger sentence becomes a rhyme so the procedure or watch-for fires on sound.
- **Automaticity reflex** — promote the couplet to the cue half of a cue→action reflex in [automaticity-and-reflex-training](./automaticity-and-reflex-training.md); the rhyme shortens cue→action latency.

## Worked example — the regulation deck

The first deck built with this method is the autistic-tuned move menu in nervous-system-regulation. One bolded couplet per move:

- **Rule** — *Don't stack the deck — pick one, then check.*
- **Deep pressure** — *When the mind's a mess, add some press.*
- **Sensory subtraction** — *Dim the scene, and the nerves go clean.*
- **Monotropic flow** — *Lock on one, the rest's undone.*
- **Stimming** — *Rock to unlock.*
- **Breath** — *Long on the out, short on the doubt.*
- **Solitude** — *Alone is the throne.*
- **Cold, carefully** — *Cool the face, slow the race.*
- **Interoception scaffold** — *Trust the seen, not the heard.*

## Measurement (METER)

Emits the same way other methods do (per [mnemonic-methods-master](./mnemonic-methods-master.md)'s operational contract):

- **Recall accuracy** — can you reproduce the couplet verbatim on cue? (Rhyme makes this near-binary, which is a feature.)
- **Cue→action latency** — because the line is also a reflex trigger, the load-bearing metric is the [automaticity](./automaticity-and-reflex-training.md) latency: time from cue to action ≤ target.
- **Floor check** — a couplet that is remembered but never *acted on* is decoration; the latency metric, not the recall metric, is the pass gate.

## Failure modes

| Failure | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Rhyme over truth** | The line scans beautifully but distorts the actual move ("rhyme-as-reason" working against you) | Meaning is the master; if the true move won't rhyme cleanly, keep it as prose — a wrong rule said well is worse than a right rule said plainly |
| **Deck inflation** | Twenty couplets; none load | A deck is ~6–10 lines max; past that, the rhymes blur into each other |
| **Decoration, not reflex** | You enjoy reciting them but never fire them at the cue | Bind each line to a real trigger via [automaticity-and-reflex-training](./automaticity-and-reflex-training.md); measure latency, not recital |
| **Cute over clear** | Cleverness obscures the action | Lead with the imperative verb; the listener should know what to *do* from the line alone |

**Mnemonic**: *Say it to weigh it* — if you'd say the line out loud, it's worth keeping.

**Checksum**: A couplet you only ever *read* failed the method — the test is whether it leaves your mouth at the cue.

**Visual**: Two playing cards laid as a couplet — the top card stamped with a **cue** glyph, the bottom card with an **action** glyph — clipped together by a bright rhyme-link, while a fanned deck of other cards sits face-down and untouched (one move, not the stack).
