## What Imagery Is

Mental imagery — also called visualization — is the ability to create and manipulate sensory experiences without external stimuli. It encompasses all senses, not just vision: sight, sound, movement, smell, taste, and touch can all be generated internally. (source: `Clippings/Exploring Cognitive Skills Imagery and Visualization.md`)

This is the substrate that every scene-based encoder in Neural OS writes to: [nedf-overview](./nedf-overview.md) Name-hook, [spear-overview](./spear-overview.md) Scene slot, [heart-overview](./heart-overview.md) face-room, and every [remaps](./remaps.md) Sensations move all require some form of internally generated sensory experience.

## The Neural Overlap Finding

When you visualize, your brain activates **the same neural pathways used in actual perception**. Imagining a visual scene activates the visual cortex just as looking at a real image does. (source: `Clippings/Exploring Cognitive Skills Imagery and Visualization.md`, citing Pearson et al. 2019, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*)

This finding is why imagery-based memory works: encoded scenes are structurally close to perceived experience, so retrieval feels like re-perception rather than abstract recall.

## The Six Imagery Channels

| Channel | Description | Strongest Neural OS application |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual** | Color, shape, spatial layout, motion | Palace scenes, NEDF Name-hook, code-glyph |
| **Auditory** | Sounds, tones, speech | REMAPS Sensations (sound), phonological encoding |
| **Kinesthetic** | Movement, body position, motor sensation | [motoric-encoding-systems](./motoric-encoding-systems.md), SPEAR Execution motor programs |
| **Olfactory** | Smell | REMAPS Sensations (smell); most distinctive sensory memory |
| **Gustatory** | Taste | REMAPS Sensations (taste); rarely primary, powerful as contrast |
| **Tactile** | Touch, texture, temperature, weight | REMAPS Sensations (touch); spatial grounding in palaces |

The kinesthetic channel is often stronger than visual in people who believe they "can't visualize." This is the entry point used in [visualization-training](./visualization-training.md) Stage 0.

## Why Vividness Matters — And Its Limits

More vivid imagery:
- Encodes faster (less repetition needed to reach recall threshold)
- Survives longer under interference
- Transfers across contexts more reliably

But **top memory athletes achieve only partial clarity on key image elements, not photographic detail** (source: AoM forum `how-to-improve-visualization-and-mental-imagery`). The operational target is not pixel-perfect imagery — it is **felt presence**: the phenomenological experience of being in or with the scene.

> "It's not exactly visual... It's more about feeling of having experience of seeing something." — Tomasz Śliwa, Art of Memory forum

This matters for encoding strategy: the REMAPS moves (especially Sensations, Exaggerate, Move) add *felt presence* more than visual resolution. That is why they work even when imagery is weak.

## The Aphantasia Spectrum

Imagery exists on a continuous scale:

| Point on spectrum | Description |
|---|---|
| **Aphantasia** | No voluntary visual mental imagery; spatial cognition preserved |
| **Weak imagery** | Vague, dim impressions; frequently unstable |
| **Average imagery** | Can form images, moderate stability, moderate vividness |
| **Vivid imagery** | Stable, controllable, multi-sensory; can manipulate at will |
| **Hyperphantasia** | Highly realistic, sometimes involuntary; can become distracting |

All points on this spectrum are **trainable**. See [memory-palace-for-aphantasia](./memory-palace-for-aphantasia.md) for the constraint-aware encoder adjustments at the low end. See [visualization-training](./visualization-training.md) for the 8-stage drill ladder (numbered on the axis at [skill-progression-stages](./skill-progression-stages.md)) that moves a user along this spectrum.

## What Imagery Is Not

**Photographic memory does not exist** in healthy adults. The only candidate ever studied (Elizabeth, Stromeyer & Psotka 1970) married the researcher and was never re-tested. Subsequent searches of over one million candidates found no verified instances (Merritt 1979). No competitor at any World Memory Championship has ever demonstrated photographic memory — all top memorizers use deliberate technique. (source: `Clippings/Eidetic Memory vs. Photographic Memory Do They Exist.md`)

**Eidetic imagery** (persistence of a visual image after the stimulus is removed) is real but rare, found almost exclusively in children (2–15% of under-12s), and is **not accurate** — eidetic images are reconstructed, not photographed. (source: same)

The practical implication: you are not training toward an impossible target. You are training a real skill that has a known ceiling well below mythology and a known floor well above "nothing."
