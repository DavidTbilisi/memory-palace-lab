## Purpose

This page extracts the math sub-skill of symbolic fluency:

- manipulate expressions accurately
- preserve notation exactness
- avoid losing scope, signs, structure, and layout under speed

## Skill Definition

```yaml
skill: symbolic fluency
skill_type: procedure
real_target: perform symbolic transformations quickly and correctly without notation collapse
real_use_case: algebra, calculus, equation solving, derivations, exams, technical work
time_horizon: 3-8 weeks
session_length: 20-40m
weekly_frequency: 5x
```

## Real Target

Zenith for this skill is not:

- understanding the concept in words only
- solving one clean exercise slowly

Zenith means:

- signs stay correct
- parentheses and scope stay intact
- equation layout remains readable
- transformations happen with low hesitation

## Core Symbolic Risks

The main fragile layers are:

- superscripts and subscripts
- fraction structure
- parentheses and scope
- sign placement
- equation layout

If these break, the idea may be understood and still be unusable.

## The physical rung (R1) — the balance substrate

*Added 2026-07-22.* This ladder used to start at Stage 0 with symbols already on paper, which assumes the operator already believes the quantity every symbolic move has to preserve: **whatever you do to one side, you do to the other**. The equality is the [invariant](./universal-mathematical-tactics.md) here, in exactly the sense that tactic page owns, and learners who have only ever been *told* the rule tend to apply it as a permission rather than as a constraint. It shows up later as sign slips and dropped terms under pressure.

The fix is a two-week physical rung below Stage 0. Draw a balance beam on paper; use algebra tiles, coins, or cups (an unknown quantity in an opaque cup, units as loose counters). Solve by physically removing the same thing from both pans. The equation is not a line of symbols to be transformed — it is a claim that two piles weigh the same, and every legal move is one that preserves the weighing.

Two honest limits, so this does not become a project:

- **Ceiling**: linear equations and simple quadratic factoring. Past that the tiles lie about what is going on. This is a two-week tool, not a practice.
- **It teaches the invariant, not the fluency.** Everything from Stage 1 onward is still symbolic work. R1 exists so the equality lands as a body fact before it lands as a rule.

Past the tiles, the substrate that carries R2–R4 is **the graph**. An equation you can picture as a shape is one you can sanity-check instantly — the visuospatial offload for algebra, playing the role the bead board plays for digits. Sketch the shape before solving, and an answer that contradicts the picture gets caught before it is used.

### The fade

Per [soroban-learning-method](./soroban-learning-method.md) §Stage 6 Scaffold Fade, promoting on accuracy and never on speed:

| Rung | What you do | Promote when |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** | Tiles on a drawn balance. Remove the same thing from both pans. | you predict the next legal move before making it, five times running |
| **R2** | Paper, narrating every move aloud: *"undo the +7, undo the ×3."* | the narration stops changing what you write |
| **R3** | Eyes on the equation, nothing written — solve by looking. | a three-step solve lands without reaching for the pen |
| **R4** | **Dictated equations** — someone reads it, you never see it. | — |

R4 is the real test and the one people skip. Reading an equation lets the page hold the state for you; hearing it does not. It is the algebra equivalent of mental soroban, and the same caution applies — do not force it before R3 is clean.

Rung and drill stage are orthogonal: you can be at Stage 4 (automaticity) on R2 and drop to R1 for a transformation family that keeps breaking. The rung answers *where does the state live*; the stage answers *how hard is the material*.

## Current-Stage Map

| Drill stage | Focus |
|---|---|
| `0 Orientation` | identify symbols, scope, and structure cleanly |
| `1 Isolation` | one symbolic move at a time |
| `2 Clean Repetition` | stable manipulation on one family of transforms |
| `3 Controlled Variation` | same idea in different symbolic surfaces |
| `4 Automaticity` | timed symbolic drills after accuracy |
| `5 Mixing` | mixed transformation families and notation noise |
| `6 Pressure And Noise` | multi-step work under time and interruption |
| `7 Transfer And Zenith` | use fluent manipulation inside real problems and derivations |

## Primary Failure Modes

- `cannot recognize` -> does not see the structure or scope correctly
- `cannot recall` -> forgets transformation rules or sign behavior
- `cannot execute` -> knows the rule but miswrites the step
- `confuses neighbors` -> similar algebraic moves blur together
- `too slow` -> manipulation is accurate but effortful
- `fails when mixed` -> several transformation types in one set break control
- `fails after disruption` -> one bad sign or bracket ruins the chain
- `fails in real conditions` -> exam pacing or notation noise causes collapse

## Stage 0: Orientation

Goal:

- read symbolic structure correctly before transforming it

Best drills:

- identify main operator
- mark scope with brackets
- name numerator, denominator, exponent, coefficient, and sign

Pass rule:

- 8 out of 10 structures parsed correctly

## Stage 1: Isolation

Goal:

- own one symbolic move at a time

Best drills:

- distribute one bracket
- combine like terms
- isolate one variable
- simplify one fraction
- move one term across the equality

Daily block:

1. 5 single-step transformations
2. check sign and scope aloud
3. rewrite cleanly

Pass rule:

- 5 correct single-step moves in a row

## Stage 2: Clean Repetition

Goal:

- stabilize one transformation family

Best drills:

- 10 one-family manipulations
- 5 layout exactness rewrites
- 5 sign-sensitive steps

Anchor drill:

- one transformation family only

Stretch drill:

- same family with different surface forms

Repair drill:

- isolate the first wrong sign, bracket, or fraction line

Pass rule:

- 9 out of 10 correct in a clean family

## Stage 3: Controlled Variation

Goal:

- keep the deep move stable when the notation changes

Best drills:

- same algebraic move in equations, fractions, and exponents
- same derivative rule under different layouts
- rewrite one expression in two notational forms

Pass rule:

- can execute the same deep move across different surfaces

## Stage 4: Automaticity

Goal:

- lower hesitation in symbolic work

Best drills:

- timed symbolic drills
- short chains of 3-5 steps
- layout-preserving rewrites under pace

Pass rule:

- accuracy stays high under moderate time pressure

Fallback rule:

- if timing causes notation collapse, return to clean repetition

## Stage 5: Mixing

Goal:

- handle mixed manipulation families

Best drills:

- mixed algebra set
- mixed calculus derivative set
- mixed sign, fraction, and exponent transformations

Pass rule:

- 80 percent or better across mixed sets without hints

## Stage 6: Pressure And Noise

Goal:

- preserve control across longer chains, time, and interruption

Best drills:

- 8-12 step chains
- restart from current line after interruption
- detect the first broken line in a corrupted derivation

Pass rule:

- can recover from a broken step without restarting the whole chain

## Stage 7: Transfer And Zenith

Goal:

- use symbolic fluency inside real math problems

Best drills:

- solve a full equation set
- derive a known result cleanly
- correct a flawed derivation
- explain why one symbolic line follows from the previous one

Pass rule:

- one real multi-step problem completed cleanly under realistic conditions

## First Two Weeks

### Week 1

- Day 1: parse structure and scope
- Day 2: one-step sign control
- Day 3: one-step bracket control
- Day 4: one-step fraction manipulation
- Day 5: one clean family set

Exit test:

- symbolic structure is read correctly
- one family is stable above 90 percent

### Week 2

- Day 1: second transformation family
- Day 2: same move, different surface forms
- Day 3: timed short set
- Day 4: mixed short set
- Day 5: one 5-step chain plus repair

Exit test:

- speed does not destroy notation
- mixed short sets stay controlled

## Minimum Daily Session

1. parse 3 expressions for scope
2. do 5 single-family manipulations
3. do 3 timed manipulations
4. repair the first wrong symbolic line

## Worked Drill-Generator Snapshot

```yaml
skill: symbolic fluency
skill_type: procedure
real_target: perform algebraic manipulations quickly and cleanly
current_stage: 2
failure_mode: cannot execute

anchor_drill:
  family: clean repetition
  prompt_shape: 10 distribution problems only
  reps: 10
  constraint: say sign and scope before writing
  pass_rule: 9/10 correct

stretch_drill:
  family: controlled variation
  prompt_shape: execute the same transformation across 5 different surface forms
  reps: 5
  constraint: preserve layout cleanly
  pass_rule: 4/5 correct

repair_drill:
  family: notation repair
  prompt_shape: identify and fix the first broken symbolic line in 3 short chains
  reps: 3
  constraint: explain the exact notation failure
  pass_rule: 3/3 repaired
```

## Reflex layer — runs beside every stage

*Added 2026-07-22.* Like the reciprocal cards in [division-drill-ladder](./division-drill-ladder.md) and the squares table in [squaring-reflexes](./squaring-reflexes.md), this layer is pure recognition and installs independently of the ladder. Its job is to remove problems from the ladder's workload rather than to make you better at climbing it.

| Card | Fires on | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Vieta** | any monic quadratic `x² + px + q` | roots sum to `−p`, multiply to `q` — factor by inspection, no formula |
| **Difference of squares** | `a² − b²` in any dress | `(a+b)(a−b)`; the same identity [squaring-reflexes](./squaring-reflexes.md) runs backwards |
| **Completing the square** | quadratic that won't factor | the standard-form move that turns any quadratic into a shifted parabola |
| **Cross-multiply** | `a/b = c/d` | `ad = bc`, one step |
| **Cramer 2×2** | two linear equations, two unknowns | memorised as a pattern, not re-derived each time |

Pass floor: the card is *named* from the equation's shape in under 2 s. Naming is the skill; executing is Stage 1 work.

The narrow Vedic equation sutras (Paravartya for simple linear forms, Śūnyaṁ Sāmyasamuccaye, Saṅkalana-Vyavakalanābhyām for special simultaneous systems) sit outside this table deliberately — their applicability conditions are fussy enough that recognising when they apply costs more than the general method saves. Worth half an hour of reading; not worth a card.

## Bottom Line

The right order here is:

1. install the invariant physically (R1)
2. parse structure
3. own one move
4. stabilize one family
5. vary the surface
6. speed up
7. mix transformations
8. recover after breaks
9. use it inside real problems, eventually without seeing them (R4)

This is the dedicated symbolic fluency ladder for Neural OS.
