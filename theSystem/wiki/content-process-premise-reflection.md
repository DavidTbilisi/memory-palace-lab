# Content · Process · Premise Reflection

**Summary**: Mezirow's three-way decomposition of critical reflection, adopted by Sloan: **content reflection** focuses on *what* (the problem's content); **process reflection** focuses on *how* (the procedures + approaches used); **premise reflection** focuses on *why* (the underlying assumptions and belief systems). Most strategy meetings ask only content questions. Process questions are rare. **Premise questions are extremely rare — and they are the ones that produce frame-shift**. The three types are layered: content is mostly surf; process bridges surf and dive; premise is deep dive.

## The three reflection types

| Type | Focus | Sample approaches | Sample questions | Domain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Content** | **What** | Restate problem · identify key issues · summarize major points · state indicators · identify factors · confirm data · verify sources | What aspect am I paying attention to? · What are the key factors? · What's the indicator? · What's the data say? | [Surf](./surf-dive-learning-domains.md) (cognitive instrumental) |
| **Process** | **How** | Map steps · explore how problem emerged · trace step-by-step actions · discuss sequence · discuss scope/impact · discuss reliability of indicators | How did this come about? · How did we get here? · How did others do it? · How exactly did the data get selected and analyzed? · How reliable are the indicators? · Would the same procedure solve this in another country? | Surf + Dive (cognitive instrumental + critical thinking) |
| **Premise** | **Why · What if** | Challenge relevance · validate inferences · challenge assumptions, beliefs, values · make rationale explicit · question the problem itself | Why is this issue even relevant to strategy? · Is this my concern? · How else could this be framed? · Is the key concept a valid concept? · Why are we measuring this? · What if the premise is wrong? | Dive (cognitive + **affective**; critical reflection + critical inquiry + critical dialogue) |

## Why premise is the high-leverage one

Sloan's observation from years of practice:

> In my practice, I have found that the questions most commonly asked in strategic sessions are content questions. Process questions require a focused and guided attention and are not asked as often. And premise questions are extremely rare.

The reason premise questions are rare:
- They feel **invasive** (challenging foundational beliefs is socially expensive)
- They have a **strong affective dimension** — they don't feel safe; people get defensive
- They lack a clean answer; they open new uncertainty
- They threaten the meeting's apparent progress (every premise question is a request to slow down)

The reason premise questions matter:
- Only premise reflection can produce **frame change** (per [six-framing-traps](./six-framing-traps.md))
- Only premise reflection exposes **invalid foundational assumptions** before they propagate into the plan
- Only premise reflection enables **innovative strategy** — extending the same premise produces only same-frame strategy

## The "twitch" — premise reflection's affective signature

Premise reflection has a distinctive somatic marker (Sloan's word: a "twitch"):

> Premise reflection consists of a strong affective dimension — we feel an unmistakable "twitch" that bolts our attention and may prompt us to resist.

When asking a premise question (or being asked one), expect:
- A momentary physical sensation of resistance
- An urge to deflect, joke, or change topic
- A short period of cognitive disorganization while the premise is being re-examined
- An impulse to defend the premise rather than examine it

The twitch is the signal that the question landed. If there is no twitch, the question was probably content or process disguised as premise.

## How the three types stack in a single inquiry round

A productive sequence on any strategic problem:

1. **Content (surf)** — establish the facts. "What's the customer churn rate this quarter?"
2. **Process (surf + dive)** — examine how the facts were obtained. "How did we measure churn? What time-window? Whose definition?"
3. **Premise (dive)** — challenge the foundation. "Why is churn the metric we care about? Are we measuring the right thing? What if the underlying premise (more customers = more revenue) is wrong here?"

Skipping content → process → premise and starting at premise lands as nihilism. Starting at content and never reaching premise lands as same-frame planning. The sequence matters.

## The 9-question dive drill maps onto these three types

The [nine-dive-question-drill](./nine-dive-question-drill.md) is a structured way to traverse content → process → premise:

| Question | Reflection type |
|---|---|
| Why? | Premise |
| Why not? | Premise |
| Whose frame? | Premise |
| Other frames? | Premise |
| Missing frames? | Premise + Process |
| What else? | Content + Process |
| Where else? | Content + Process |
| When else? | Content + Process |
| How else? | Process |

Six of the nine are premise-loaded — by design. The drill is biased toward the rare and high-leverage type.

## METER

- `premise_question_count` per session — floor 1, pass ≥3 for strategic-thinking sessions
- `twitch_logged` — count of premise questions that produced detectable resistance; pass ≥1 per session
- `content_only_session_flag` — flag sessions with zero premise questions; pass <10% of sessions

## Failure modes

1. **Content-only sessions** — premise never surfaces; team thinks they're "strategic" but they're planning with frozen premises. Most common failure.
2. **Process-only sessions** — team examines *how* without ever asking *why* the activity itself matters. Produces optimization of the wrong process.
3. **Premature premise** — opening with premise questions before establishing content; lands as nihilism / posturing. Resolution: traverse content → process → premise in order.
4. **Performance-premise** — asking premise questions to look thoughtful while protecting the actual premise from challenge. Detected by: no twitch, no follow-through, returns to content-mode answers.

## Mnemonic

A **stratified well** in Velvet Aeon Environment-mode. At the **surface ring**, a clear pool reflects the sky — a **young scholar** kneels at the rim with a notebook, recording numbers and names (content reflection — *what*). Halfway down, a **woman in working clothes** descends a wooden ladder, hand-tracing each rung, examining the cracks in the well-wall to map *how* the well was dug (process reflection — *how*). At the deepest, where the light fails entirely, sits a **third figure** — a long-haired woman in a stark white shift, **head tilted upward**, asking the well itself: *"why are you here? what if you don't need to be?"* (premise reflection — *why*). The deepest figure has the fragile/luminous face. The well's stone walls are **carved with three rings** at each level — one for each type — visible from above as concentric circles. A small, almost-invisible **twitch-glow** lights up the deepest figure's collarbone when her question lands; the scholar at the surface looks down, startled.
