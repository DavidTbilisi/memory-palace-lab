# Visual Thinking — The Evidence Base (and Its Boundary)

**Summary**: The empirical grounding for the wiki's visual layer, and the falsifier that keeps it honest. The "visual thinking is to verbal thinking as the ocean is to a drop" intuition is **true about visual *perception* and visual *memory* (both genuinely ocean-scale) and false about visual *thinking* itself** (internal imagery/reasoning is bottlenecked at ~10 bits/s like all cognition). The high bandwidth lives in the eye and in recognition memory, not in the mind's eye. This page supplies the verified studies behind the [visual-per-concept rule](./feedback-visual-per-concept.md) and [show-vs-say](./show-vs-say.md), quarantines the debunked "visual learner" myth that sits one inch away from the claim, and refines the rule from a **quota** (every concept ships a visual) to a **quality gate** (every concept that admits a *form-sharing* visual ships one; manufactured decoration is banned).

## The one-line verdict

The metaphor is **true about perception and memory, false about thinking, and it sits next to a dead myth.** Width = bandwidth: the eye and recognition memory are oceans; internal visual cognition is a drop bottlenecked like everything else. Encode the ocean; never claim the drop is one.

## Where "ocean vs drop" is genuinely true

Three independent literatures back the strong form — these are real, large, and primary-sourced:

1. **Perceptual bandwidth.** The optic nerve carries ~10 Mbit/s (Koch et al. 2006); the photoreceptor array ~1.6 Gbit/s (Zheng & Meister 2024). Conscious/behavioral throughput is ~**10 bits/s** (Hick 1952; Miller 1956; Zheng & Meister 2024). A gap of 6–8 orders of magnitude — ocean vs drop *understates* it.
2. **Visual memory is effectively unbounded.** Single exposure, two-alternative recognition: **98% for pictures vs 90% for words** (Shepard 1967). Shown 10,000 pictures once, people retained **~6,600** two days later — "almost limitless" (Standing 1973). Plain verbal storage has nothing close.
3. **Spatial encoding is trainable and powerful.** Memory champions aren't smarter or built differently — 9/10 use the method of loci, with no IQ or structural-brain difference (Maguire et al. 2003). Six weeks of loci training moved novices **+35 words vs +7–11 for controls, durable at 4 months** (Dresler et al. 2017). This is the evidence under [rubiks-cube-palace](./rubiks-cube-palace.md) and [memory-palace-architecture-for-neural-os](./memory-palace-architecture-for-neural-os.md).

## Where the metaphor overreaches — the boundary

The huge numbers all belong to **perception** (intake) and **storage** (recognition). The moment cognition itself is measured — reasoning, planning, manipulating an internal image — it collapses to the same ~10 bits/s bottleneck as everything else (Zheng & Meister 2024, the "inner brain"). The vivid fullness you *feel* in mental imagery is precisely what Nørretranders named the **"user illusion"** — subjective richness, not measured throughput.

So the honest form is: **visual intake and visual memory are an ocean; visual *thinking* is bottlenecked like all thinking.** (Footnote on provenance: the famous "40 bits/s conscious" figure is itself drift — Nørretranders wrote ~16, and the modern estimate is ~10. The figure's instability is the tell.)

The metaphor's word **"thinking" is the load-bearing error.** The framework's power comes from intake and memory, not from a claim that thought is pictorial.

## Thinking tool vs memory tool — the 2×2 (and why automaticity doesn't collapse it)

A natural next question: *is a visual a thinking tool or a memory tool?* The answer is "both, but in different cells." The split is **not** memory-vs-thinking; it is two other axes — **where** the visual lives (on the page / in your head) and **what** it carries (structure-relations / vivid pictorial detail).

![Thinking tool vs memory tool — the 2×2](assets/visual-thinking-2x2.svg)

| | **Structure / relations** | **Vivid pictorial detail** |
|---|---|---|
| **On the page** (external) | ★ **Thinking tool** — diagrams, graphs, NIPSF Instrument layer; offloads working memory and **recruits perception** (the ocean) to see structure in parallel | ⚠ **Conditional** — an aid *only if content-bearing* (multimedia effect for novices); decorative/redundant is net-negative (d < 0.00) |
| **In your head** (internal) | ◆ **Spatial reasoning + loci** — mental models, method of loci, palaces, [CAST](./cast-overview.md); does *not* impede reasoning (Knauff 2002); automaticity makes this cell cheap | ★ **Memory tool** — vivid dual-coded images for recall (Shepard/Standing); a **trap as a reasoning *engine*** (~10 bits/s, can impede) |

The diagonal is the whole point: **visual-as-thinking lives top-left** (external + structural), **visual-as-memory lives bottom-right** (internal + vivid). The one move the evidence forbids is narrow — running your reasoning *inside* a vivid mental picture, not thinking *with* visuals at all.

### Does automaticity (palaces · loci · CAST) replace paper?

Partly — and the boundary is precise. Automaticity changes the **cost** of using the internal cells, not their **bandwidth** or **function**:

- **What it replaces.** For *mastered, recurring* structure — recall and execution of what you've already encoded — automatic internal tools genuinely displace paper. A fluent palace recalls a speech without notes; an automatic [mental abacus](./soroban-learning-method.md) does arithmetic without scratch paper; a drilled [CAST](./cast-overview.md) graph is walked from memory. Driving internal tools to automaticity (the [automaticity ladder](./skill-progression-stages.md)) *is* the way to reduce paper dependence for consolidated skill. That half of your intuition is right.
- **What it cannot replace.** Paper's leverage as a *thinking* tool was never a memory advantage, so making memory automatic doesn't touch it. Three offload advantages persist no matter how trained you are:
  1. **It recruits perception.** A diagram on the page is read by your visual *perception* — the ~10⁷ bits/s channel (the ocean). An internal image, however automatic, is replayed into the *serial ~10 bits/s conscious workspace* (the drop). Externalizing is the only way to put structure **in front of the ocean**; the mind's eye can't show your perception a picture. This is the same reason [aphantasia](./memory-palace-for-aphantasia.md) doesn't cripple reasoning — and the reason paper survives.
  2. **It holds novel state at zero maintenance cost.** Thinking is manipulation of *novel, in-flux* configurations. Holding a novel structure in your head taxes working memory *while* you manipulate it, and manipulation degrades the held image. Paper holds it perfectly for free. Automaticity helps you retrieve *known* structures cheaply; it gives no discount on maintaining a structure that doesn't exist yet.
  3. **It makes errors visible.** Your head will confabulate its own consistency; the page can be wrong in a way you can *see* ([show-vs-say](./show-vs-say.md): "prose can lie about its own structure; the diagram can't").

So: **internal automaticity replaces paper for retrieving and executing mastered structure; paper remains irreducible for reasoning over novel structure** — because that work belongs to the external-structural cell, and no amount of memory training moves it into perception. (The honest edge case: heavily-drilled experts — blindfold chess, mental calculators — *do* manipulate over-learned internal representations with real fluency, but only within a narrow, regular domain, and the externalization advantage reappears the moment the structure is novel or large. The exception proves the rule.)

## The myth that must stay quarantined

One foot from the claim sits the **learning-styles / "visual learner"** construct, and it is dead:

- Matching instruction to a person's "visual style" yields **no learning benefit** — it fails its own falsification test (Pashler et al. 2008; Massa & Mayer 2006; Willingham 2018). 89% of educators believe it anyway (Newton & Salvi 2020); it runs on folklore.
- **Aphantasia** is the clean disproof: ~2–3% of people have little or no voluntary mental imagery yet reason normally and **over-cluster into STEM** — Craig Venter, Ed Catmull (Pixar), Blake Ross (Firefox) (Zeman et al. 2015, 2020). Vivid imagery is *not required* for high-level thought. (This is why [memory-palace-for-aphantasia](./memory-palace-for-aphantasia.md) and [rubiks-cube-palace](./rubiks-cube-palace.md) are first-class, not afterthoughts.)
- Visual imagery can actively **impede** deductive reasoning, and the impedance vanishes in congenitally blind reasoners — isolating *visual* imagery as the culprit (Knauff & Johnson-Laird 2002; Knauff & May 2006). Pylyshyn (1973) supplies the theoretical case that thought is fundamentally propositional.
- The "we remember 10%/20%/30%… of what we read/hear/see" retention pyramid and "65% of people are visual learners" are **fabricated** — no primary source, ever (corrupted Dale's Cone; Subramony et al. 2014; Thalheimer: "virtually zero chance" the percentages are correct).

If the framework's pitch ever rests on "people are visual learners," anyone who knows the literature discredits the whole thing in one sentence. The claim is about **the encoding**, not **the learner type** — it stays there.

## The boundary statement (what we claim / what we don't)

> **Neural OS claims:**
> - Visual *perception* is the highest-bandwidth channel into the mind — orders of magnitude above conscious throughput.
> - Visual and spatial *memory* have effectively unbounded capacity, vastly exceeding rote verbal storage (Shepard 1967; Standing 1973).
> - *Dual-coded* encoding — image **+** word **+** spatial location — produces large, durable, trainable retention gains (Paivio; Dresler et al. 2017; method of loci).
> - Therefore every concept that admits a *form-sharing* visual gets one, because structure can be **shown** and not merely **said** ([show-vs-say](./show-vs-say.md), TLP 4.1212).
>
> **Neural OS does *not* claim:**
> - **Not** that "visual thinking" is high-bandwidth — internal imagery/reasoning is bottlenecked at ~10 bits/s like all cognition; the felt richness is the user illusion, not throughput.
> - **Not** that people are "visual learners," or that matching material to a learner's style improves learning — that hypothesis fails its own test (Pashler et al. 2008), and vivid imagery isn't even required for high-level thought (aphantasia; Zeman 2015/2020).
> - **Not** that more visuals are always better — decorative, redundant, and seductive images are net-negative (Levin et al. 1987; Mayer redundancy; Rey 2012). The rule is visual *relevance and integration*, not visual *count*.
> - **Not** that the famous "we remember X% of what we see" numbers are real — they are fabricated folklore (Subramony et al. 2014).

This block is the canonical wording for the top of any curriculum module, book chapter, or public-facing Red Queen artifact (see red-queen-academy). Quote it; do not paraphrase it loose.

## The quota → quality refinement for the visual-per-concept rule

The owner page [feedback-visual-per-concept](./feedback-visual-per-concept.md) already carries a quality dimension via [show-vs-say](./show-vs-say.md): a visual must *share form*, and "picture as decoration" is a named failure mode. The multimedia-learning evidence **quantifies and validates** that stance, and adds one boundary the rule was missing:

- **Decoration is not neutral filler — it is net-negative.** Illustrations promoting deep processing run d > 0.50; decorative illustrations run **d < 0.00** (Levin, Anglin & Carney 1987, via Mayer 2011). Redundant added media *hurt* (removing them: d = 0.72; Mayer redundancy effect). Interesting-but-irrelevant visuals depress retention and transfer (Rey 2012, d ≈ −0.30 / −0.48). Field-level pooled multimedia effect is only **g = 0.37** (Cromley & Chen 2025), far below headline lab medians.
- **The missing boundary:** a strict quota ("*every* concept must ship a visual") manufactures decoration *by policy* whenever a concept admits no form-sharing visual. So the binary `has_visual` gate must be **subordinate** to `visual_shows_form`. **A page with no visual beats a page with a decorative one.**
- **Expertise reversal:** the same visual that helps a novice can hurt an expert (Kalyuga et al. 1998, 2003). For a wiki re-read as the reader's mastery climbs the [automaticity ladder](./skill-progression-stages.md), the visual should degrade gracefully (integrated-text for novices → diagram-alone for experts), not sit fixed.

Net: the rule is **keep-with-modification**. The intent (show, don't just say) is right and now empirically armored; the *quota* phrasing is the drift. Reframe: *"every concept that admits a content-bearing, form-sharing, integrated visual must ship one; decorative or quota-filler visuals are banned, and no visual is preferable to a decorative one."*

## METER integration

The owner page's hooks feed [METER](./meter-overview.md) (the wiki's measurement layer); this page re-orders them so quality gates count, not mere presence:

| Event | Fires when | Polarity |
|---|---|---|
| `concept.visual_shows_form` | the visual's form mirrors the concept's structure (the show-vs-say test) — **primary gate** | Higher = better |
| `concept.has_visual` | a visual is present at all — **subordinate**; only counts if `visual_shows_form` passes | Neutral unless paired |
| `concept.decoration_by_mandate` | a visual was added to satisfy the quota but fails the form test | **Lower = better** (a defect signal) |

Proposed floor: **zero `concept.decoration_by_mandate` events** across the concept corpus — a non-zero count means the quota is manufacturing the exact net-negative visuals the evidence warns against.

## Mnemonic

**EYE ≠ I.** The **EYE** (perception + recognition memory) is the ocean; the **I** (the conscious self that thinks, images, reports) is the drop. Same three letters of difference as the claim: keep the bandwidth where it actually lives.

Memory image: a vast blue ocean pouring through the pupil of a giant eye, funnelling down into a single trembling **drop** hanging off the word "I" — and beside the drop, a small sign reading *"visual learner"* crossed out in red, half-sunk in the sand.

## Memory checksum

If you can answer these in <60 s each from memory, the page is encoded:

1. **Which two things are the ocean, and which one is the drop?** (Ocean = visual *perception* + visual/spatial *memory*; drop = visual *thinking*/imagery/reasoning, ~10 bits/s.)
2. **Name the two recognition-memory numbers.** (Shepard 1967: 98% pictures vs 90% words; Standing 1973: ~6,600 of 10,000 retained.)
3. **Why is the "ocean vs drop" headline partly apples-to-oranges?** (It compares a *sensory channel capacity* against a *behavioral throughput* — different units, different stages. Perception is the firehose; report is the trickle.)
4. **State the one myth to quarantine and its disproof.** ("Visual learner"/learning-styles — fails the crossover-interaction test (Pashler 2008); aphantasics reason fine and cluster into STEM.)
5. **State the quota→quality refinement in one sentence.** (Every concept that admits a *form-sharing* visual ships one; decoration is banned; no visual beats a decorative one — because decorative images are net-negative, not neutral.)
6. **Thinking tool or memory tool — and does automaticity replace paper?** (Both, on a diagonal: thinking = external+structural, memory = internal+vivid. Automaticity makes the *internal* cells cheap and replaces paper for *mastered recall/execution* — but paper is irreducible for *novel structure*, because only externalizing puts structure in front of perception.)

If the answer to (1) ever puts the high bandwidth in "thinking," (4) treats "visual learner" as real, or (6) claims automaticity replaces paper for *novel* reasoning, the page has drifted.
