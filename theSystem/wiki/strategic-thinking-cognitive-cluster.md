# Strategic Thinking Cognitive Cluster

**Summary**: The 13-trait **divergent / a-rational / generative** cluster that supports the Thinking dot of the [sloan-triad-model](./sloan-triad-model.md), and the 9-trait **convergent / analytical / rational** cluster that supports Planning + Implementation. Naming the clusters separately is the load-bearing move: most "strategy" training trains only the convergent cluster, then complains there are no strategic thinkers. The clusters are **complementary**, not competing; and each cluster can **actively undermine** the other if mis-applied.

**Sources**:
- [sloan-learning-to-think-strategically](./sloan-learning-to-think-strategically.md) — source summary
- Sloan, *Learning to Think Strategically* (4th ed., 2020), Ch 4 §"Cognitive Clusters for Strategic Thinking and Strategic Planning™", Figure 4.1

## The two clusters side-by-side

| Trait family | Strategic Thinking | Strategic Planning |
|---|---|---|
| **Direction** | Divergent | Convergent |
| **Logic** | A-rational | Rational |
| **Abstraction** | Conceptual · Abstract · Symbolic | Concrete · Literal |
| **Linearity** | Nonlinear · Generative · Iterative | Linear · Sequential · Stepwise |
| **Scope** | Panoramic · Polarity | Reductionist |
| **Source of decision** | Intuitive · Metaphoric · Creative | Analytical · Objective · Logical |
| **Affective involvement** | High · Affect is load-bearing | Low · Affect is noise |
| **Reflective processes** | Critical inquiry · Critical reflection · Critical dialogue · Critical thinking | Critical thinking only (the others are out of scope) |

The Thinking cluster lists 13 named traits; the Planning cluster lists 9. The asymmetry is by design — Planning is well-specified by its rationality discipline; Thinking requires multiple complementary moves because no single move suffices to suspend solution-finding.

## Why the two-cluster framing matters

### Most "strategy" training trains only the Planning cluster

Business schools, consulting firm methods, executive education programs — overwhelmingly oriented to convergent, analytical, model-based thinking. The graduates are excellent planners and weak strategists. Sloan's interviews with executives:

> Most of what I learned in courses was only technique — like a nice craft. (Japanese financial executive)

> I tell [junior executives] they must talk to many people and read everything. And I tell them not to worry about learning about the models of strategy. (laughter) (German manufacturing executive)

### Each cluster can undermine the other

| Mis-application | Failure mode |
|---|---|
| Bringing **convergent / reductionist** thinking into a strategic-thinking session | Collapses divergence prematurely; team "solves" a wrongly-framed problem |
| Bringing **divergent / a-rational** thinking into a strategic-planning session | Plan stays vague, unmeasurable, unimplementable |
| Bringing **affective / intuitive** thinking into implementation | Project drifts; team second-guesses commitments mid-execution |
| Bringing **reductionist** thinking into the affective half of preparation | Kills the must-win drive that powers the [3-stage learning](./sloan-three-stage-learning-model.md) cycle |

The leader's discipline: **state which cluster the team should run, signal it explicitly, and re-state when the dot shifts**.

### The cluster is what makes informal learning the right learning mode

The Thinking cluster traits (intuitive, metaphoric, polarity, panoramic, a-rational) are **not teachable** in the formal sense. They are *learnable* — through experience, prior successful life experience, dialogue, the arts, travel. This is why [the 3-stage learning model](./sloan-three-stage-learning-model.md) is informal: the cluster requires informal substrate.

## The a-rational distinction (Jung)

Sloan invokes Jung's distinction: **a-rational** is not the same as **irrational**.

> Intuition does not denote something contrary to reason, but something outside the province of reason. (Carl Jung)

- *Rational* = within the rules of formal logic and explicit inference
- *Irrational* = violating those rules
- *A-rational* = *outside* their domain — using a different kind of knowing (tacit, pattern-based, embodied)

Strategic thinking is *a-rational*, not irrational. This distinction matters because dismissive critics ("strategy by gut feeling is just irrational") are conflating the two. The wiki adopts Jung's distinction and Sloan's framing.

## Mnemonic

A **Velvet Aeon mirror** with **two faces**. The left face shows a woman in an architect's apron with a **steel ruler, compass, and ledger**, scribing **clean parallel lines** toward a single inked point (Planning cluster — convergent). The right face shows the same woman in **flowing scarves of mixed color**, **eyes closed**, **hands cradling a swirling galaxy of small objects** (a feather, a coin, a fish, a flame — incongruent, polar, panoramic) (Thinking cluster — divergent + a-rational). Between the two faces, the mirror's silver **vibrates**, a faint hum — the clusters can interfere when both demand the foreground. A small caption above the mirror reads, in Velvet-Aeon glyph: **"a-rational ≠ irrational"** (the Jung distinction). The woman's hair is long and lifted on both sides; the lighting is single-source warm (one moon, not two), per feedback_image_noise_reduction.
