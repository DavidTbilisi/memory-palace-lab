## What HEART Solves

HEART is designed for repeated interactions with important people such as children, students, family, and colleagues. Its aim is not just recall of isolated traits, but a falsifiable working model of how the person tends to react, what shaped them, and what treatment works. (source: HEART_OVERVIEW.md; MULTIDIMENSIONAL_vs_UNIDIMENSIONAL_ENCODING.md)

HEART stands for:

- **History**
- **Essence**
- **Architecture**
- **Recognition**
- **Treatment**

The result is a person-specific room in a larger people palace. (source: HEART_OVERVIEW.md; PEOPLE_PALACE_STRUCTURE.md)

## Core Layout

Each person gets a stable room with a fixed layout:

- **Doorway** = Recognition
- **Left wall** = History
- **Center** = Essence
- **Right wall** = Architecture
- **Back wall** = Treatment
- **Ceiling** = Pattern tag

This room structure is the main organizing principle of HEART. (source: HEART_OVERVIEW.md; PEOPLE_PALACE_STRUCTURE.md)

## HEART as a Tag Stack

HEART is easiest to understand when expressed as a [UMTF](./universal-mental-tagging-framework.md) stack.

### Phase 1 Tag Stack

Phase 1 is the minimal fast version for usable prediction. (source: HEART_OVERVIEW.md; HEART_PHASE1_RAPID_PROFILING.md)

- **Spatial**: fixed person-room layout inside the people palace
- **Sensory**: face exaggeration, name sound-hook, voice/posture signature at the doorway
- **State**: temperament shown in the center totem and immediate current feel
- **Pattern**: one repeating pattern tag on the ceiling
- **Relation**: one dominant trigger → reaction → relief loop on the right wall
- **Priority**: only important people get full rooms; tertiary people may get pattern tags only

### Phase 2 Tag Stack

Phase 2 deepens the model for people who matter most. (source: HEART_OVERVIEW.md; HEART_PHASE2_DEEPENING.md; PEOPLE_PALACE_STRUCTURE.md)

- **Spatial**: same room, but fully populated across all walls
- **Sensory**: reinforced doorway and scene-level vividness across key memories
- **State**: richer essence model and more precise current-condition cues
- **Pattern**: reusable person-type and behavioral pattern classification
- **Relation**: expanded behavioral architecture with branches, appraisals, and causal loops
- **Temporal**: formative history arranged chronologically on the left wall; recent confirmations lower on the wall
- **Priority**: strongest detail reserved for top-value relationships and highest-risk interpretations

### Practical Reading

HEART is therefore not a generic trait list. Its dominant tag family is:

**Spatial + Sensory + Pattern + Relation + Priority**, with **Temporal** becoming much more important in Phase 2. (source: HEART_OVERVIEW.md; PEOPLE_PALACE_STRUCTURE.md; MULTIDIMENSIONAL_vs_UNIDIMENSIONAL_ENCODING.md)

## Why HEART Is Multidimensional

The raw materials explicitly argue that list-style encoding is weak because it lacks spatial structure, interaction between dimensions, predictive power, revision discipline, and pattern reuse. HEART solves this by distributing different kinds of information onto different locations and cue types in the room. (source: MULTIDIMENSIONAL_vs_UNIDIMENSIONAL_ENCODING.md)

That means:

- recognition is immediate at the doorway
- causality lives on the right wall
- explanatory history lives on the left wall
- treatment rules live on the back wall
- reusable pattern identity lives on the ceiling

Each location answers a different question, which is exactly why HEART fits the UMTF orthogonality rule. (source: PEOPLE_PALACE_STRUCTURE.md; MULTIDIMENSIONAL_vs_UNIDIMENSIONAL_ENCODING.md)

## Phase 1 Minimal Content

Phase 1 stores only the minimum needed for usable control:

- doorway: face + name sound-hook
- center: 3-axis animal or essence-lite totem
- right wall: one dominant behavioral loop
- ceiling: one pattern tag

This is intentionally sparse and fast. (source: HEART_OVERVIEW.md; HEART_PHASE1_RAPID_PROFILING.md)

For a first-time stranger, the doorway payload itself (face + name sound-hook) is produced by [name-face-fast-encode](./name-face-fast-encode.md) — a sub-5-second Tier-2 primitive that fuses the name with one prominent feature into a single Distinguisher-bearing hybrid word. It never builds a full room on its own; the falsifiable promotion gate is that the person must recur before the fast-encode is expanded into a proper HEART room.

## Phase 2 Full Content

Phase 2 adds:

- left wall: formative history scenes
- fuller essence model
- expanded architecture with branches and appraisals
- back wall treatment rules
- falsification discipline for architecture claims

This turns the room into a deeper and more testable model rather than a quick profile. (source: HEART_OVERVIEW.md; HEART_PHASE2_DEEPENING.md; HEART_SAFETY_PROTOCOL.md)

## Why Pattern Tags Matter

The ceiling tag is the speed multiplier. It allows transfer across people: "explosion-regret," "freeze-avoid," "attention-seeker," and similar recurring structures become reusable chunks. Instead of re-learning every person from scratch, you match them to a known pattern and then refine. (source: HEART_OVERVIEW.md; PATTERN_LIBRARY_TEMPLATE.md; PEOPLE_PALACE_STRUCTURE.md)

## Safety Constraint

HEART explicitly requires falsification for behavioral interpretations. Architecture claims should be framed so that contradictory observations would force revision. This keeps the model from collapsing into projection or vague storytelling. (source: HEART_OVERVIEW.md; HEART_SAFETY_PROTOCOL.md)

## Recognition slot — 4-axis efficacy rubric (added 2026-05-27)

The Doorway/Recognition slot now carries an optional **4-axis efficacy rubric** beside the face/voice/name cues. The rubric is a 30–60 s structured read populated on first meeting and revised on every subsequent interaction. Owner page: interlocutor-efficacy-rubric. The four axes:

1. **Energy level** — gestures, speech rate, gaze (raw output)
2. **Energy structure** — clarity of logic, balance, consistency (channeled output)
3. **Social maturity** — decisions taken, responsibility owned (upstream)
4. **Social adequacy** — listening, asking, room-reading (downstream of maturity)

Plus a **values-attractor** read (material / authority / power / information / other) as a starting hypothesis for what the person organizes attention around.

**Caveats** (load-bearing): (a) Tier-3 heuristic, not a measurement; never load-bearing for high-stakes calls. (b) For I-type subjects, the rubric reads the auxiliary not the dominant — keep `aux-view` flag set until `dom-confirmed`. (c) State (stress, illness, jet-lag) suppresses all four axes; defer the read if [PULSE](./pulse-overview.md) flags high stress. (d) The Greenberg attribution to "A. Bandura" has been **falsified via literature check (2026-05-27)** — rubric does not appear in Bandura's *Self-Efficacy* (1997) or *Social Foundations* (1986). Upstream source unverified — likely Russian popular-psychology repackaging. Cite the rubric as a Greenberg-sourced operational heuristic, not as Bandura's framework. See interlocutor-efficacy-rubric §Sources for the full citation discipline.

## Optional GRACE Integration: `social_style` field

A HEART room can carry an optional `social_style` field listing person-specific [GRACE](./grace-overview.md) move preferences for that individual — for example, "Maria responds well to Apology mode at position 2-3, never 4-5" or "this person reads position-5 deference as performative." This is a Phase 3 extension rather than a Phase 1 or Phase 2 requirement; populate only when GRACE is in active use and a specific person's calibration deviates from the general gradient. The schema is deliberately loose pending real use.

## MBTI constraint — introvert subjects show their auxiliary, not their dominant

For introvert subjects (any I-type per MBTI), the **first behavior you observe is the auxiliary function, not the dominant**. This is the general-in-tent metaphor: extraverts' dominant is always on view; introverts' dominant stays in the tent and the auxiliary handles outside-world traffic.

Operational consequence for HEART:

- **Doorway recognition** for an I-type subject captures the *auxiliary* — their public face, the function they show to first-meeting strangers and to anyone who hasn't been admitted past the tent flap. This is real data but it is **not** the center of who they are.
- **Center essence** for an I-type subject should be marked as **provisional until the dominant is observed**. Their actual essence lives inside their dominant function, which surfaces only when the topic genuinely matters to them, when they're alone with someone they trust, or in their work output rather than their conversational presence.
- **Architecture loops** for an I-type may look reasonable when modeled from the auxiliary alone — and then surprise you when the dominant takes over. A common failure mode: building an INTJ's room from their visible Te (organized, decisive, executive-feeling) and being unable to predict the long Ni-driven pivots they make every few years.

The fix:

1. **Mark the room as `aux-view` until you've seen the dominant.** A flag in the HEART card honoring the structural fact that you've encoded only half the person. Promote to `dom-confirmed` only after observing the dominant in operation (typically: extended one-on-one time on a topic they care about, or sustained work output that exposes the dominant's signature).
2. **For known I-type subjects, use the dom/aux pair as the Phase 1 essence** rather than a single essence-totem. Two functions in the center — one labeled (dominant, hidden), one labeled (auxiliary, shown) — gives a more honest model from the start.
3. **For extravert subjects this constraint does not apply.** What you see is what they are; no aux-view flag needed.

This amendment was added 2026-05-24 from the MBTI ingest. The wiki's prior HEART practice implicitly assumed dominant-on-display for everyone, which produced systematically distorted rooms for the ~38-40% of the population who are introverts.

## Aphantasia constraint

The default HEART pattern places a person in a *room with a portrait* — the face is one of the strongest identity cues. Under the aphantasia constraint, face imagery is absent or weak and the portrait-based recognition pattern breaks. Compensating moves: **Architecture** does heavier work (relational position — who they sit next to, who they answer to, what they own — recovers identity better than face), **Treatment** becomes the load-bearing identity cue (the rule-of-conduct for this person is more recoverable than visual furnishings), and **Recognition** uses voice clip, characteristic phrase, name-as-text in a known typeface, signature gait, or hand-shape rather than face. **History** and **Essence** are unchanged because they are propositional. See [memory-palace-for-aphantasia](./memory-palace-for-aphantasia.md) §"Notes for HEART users" for the full re-weighting.

## Mnemonic

**Walk the room, not the letters.** Each person's room has a fixed layout, and the spatial order *is* the retrieval order: you enter at the **doorway — Recognition**, turn to the **left wall — History**, face **Essence** at the centre, the **right wall — Architecture**, the **back wall — Treatment**, and the **ceiling** carries the reusable pattern tag. Five walls and a ceiling; if a wall is blank you have not met the person yet.

## Checksum

1. Walk the room: name what each of the five walls and the ceiling hold.
2. HEART requires Architecture claims to be framed a particular way. What way, and what does that protect against?
3. Which slot sits at the doorway, and why is that the one you meet first?
