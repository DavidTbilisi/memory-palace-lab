# REMAPS

> **Stage 1 of [image-pipeline](./image-pipeline.md)** — REMAPS is the transformation half (Stage 1); [CLAMP](./clamp-render-lens.md) is the render-direction half (Stage 2). See [image-pipeline](./image-pipeline.md) for the unified workflow.

**Summary**: Six-move transformation checklist — Rotate · Exaggerate · Modify-Merge-Move · Associate · Play-Palace-Path · Sensations — for making any image, concept, or scene retrievable. REMAPS sits underneath every encoder; when material is too abstract, weak, ordinary, or static to stick, the six moves supply the dimensions to re-map it into a form the mind can hold, manipulate, and recover.

## What REMAPS Is

REMAPS is a transformation framework, not an encoder. It does not decide *what* gets encoded ([NEDF](./nedf-overview.md) handles concepts, [CAST](./cast-overview.md) handles graphs, [SPEAR](./spear-overview.md) handles procedures, etc.) — it decides *how to make the encoding stick* once a slot has been filled. The name is the purpose: literally re-mapping material into a form the mind can hold and retrieve. (source: raw/Neural OS Book/REMAPS.md)

Reach for REMAPS when an image, concept, or scene is failing one of these tests:

- too abstract — no concrete sensory hook
- too weak — recallable only with effort
- too ordinary — no distinctive feature against neighbors
- too static — no motion, no temporal structure

(source: raw/Neural OS Book/REMAPS.md)

## The Six Moves

### R — Rotate / Reverse / Relocate

Change orientation, invert the normal relation, or move the image into a stronger place / put it on a route. (source: raw/Neural OS Book/REMAPS.md)

Typical applications:
- **Rotate** the image upside-down or sideways for distinctiveness
- **Reverse** cause and effect, or swap roles between subject and object
- **Relocate** to a palace locus with stronger emotional or spatial association

### E — Exaggerate / Eliminate

Intensify the important feature, or strip away unnecessary parts so the signal becomes clearer. (source: raw/Neural OS Book/REMAPS.md)

Typical applications:
- **Exaggerate** scale (100× the critical element), brightness, loudness, motion
- **Eliminate** background detail; isolate the single feature that carries the meaning

### M — Modify / Merge / Move

Change shape, scale, texture, or behavior; merge two images; add motion and collision. (source: raw/Neural OS Book/REMAPS.md)

Typical applications:
- **Modify** texture or material (metal → flesh, solid → translucent, dry → wet)
- **Merge** two pegs into one impossible object (the binding step in [peg-matrix-remaps-scenes](./peg-matrix-remaps-scenes.md))
- **Move** with active verbs (flying, dripping, swinging, crashing) — static images are weakly retrievable

### A — Associate / Adapt / Aesthetic

Bind the target to a known anchor, adapt a familiar pattern, or make it striking enough to notice. (source: raw/Neural OS Book/REMAPS.md)

Typical applications:
- **Associate** with an existing palace, person, or icon already drilled
- **Adapt** a familiar structural pattern (hub-spoke, cascade, bottleneck) to the new scene
- **Aesthetic** — beauty or grotesqueness; anything that captures attention beyond neutral

### P — Play / Palace / Path

Add humor, absurdity, or fun; give the image an address in a room, route, or fixed spatial path. (source: raw/Neural OS Book/REMAPS.md)

Typical applications:
- **Play** — comic exaggeration, slapstick collisions, cartoonish reactions
- **Palace** — dock the scene at a fixed locus inside an existing palace
- **Path** — place along a route so order is preserved by traversal direction

### S — Sensations / Symbols

Add touch, sound, smell, taste, temperature, texture; or symbolic shorthand when useful. (source: raw/Neural OS Book/REMAPS.md)

Typical applications:
- **Sensations** — heat, stickiness, weight, smell, ambient sound (these are the [UMTF](./universal-mental-tagging-framework.md) Sensory subchannels in their richest form)
- **Symbols** — glyphs, color codes, marks that compress meaning at a glance

**Aphantasia caveat.** The Sensations move is the only REMAPS move that depends on the user being able to evoke vivid sensory imagery on demand. It is weak or unavailable for the ~26% of aphantasics with multi-sensory imagery deficit (the cognitive profile described in Dawes et al. 2020 covering sound, touch, motion, taste, smell, and emotion). Recommended substitutions for that subgroup: spatial-scale exaggeration (the *bed is the milk*, not "milk smells strong"), [bilateral contrast](./bilateral-contrast-system.md) in place of synaesthetic binding, and motoric markers ([finger-procedure-system](./finger-procedure-system.md), [palm-zone-category-system](./palm-zone-category-system.md)) in place of sensory tags. R, E, M, A, P remain fully available. See [memory-palace-for-aphantasia](./memory-palace-for-aphantasia.md) for the full per-encoder reweighting.

## What REMAPS Absorbs

REMAPS is the umbrella for three previously-scattered families of mnemonic moves: (source: raw/Neural OS Book/REMAPS.md)

- **Creative variation**: substitute, combine, adapt, modify, eliminate, reverse
- **Vividness**: exaggeration, color, symbolism, beauty, humor
- **Spatial indexing**: palace, path, relocation

Combined into one practical checklist that covers transformation, vividness, and indexing in a single pass.

Historically, REMAPS is the 12→6 compression of Tony Buzan's [SMASHIN' SCOPE](./smashin-scope.md) — the 12-principle memory protocol Buzan synthesized from the classical Greek *ars memoriae* and mid-20th-century imagery research. SMASHIN' SCOPE's 12 letters (Synaesthesia · Movement · Association · Sexuality · Humour · Imagination · Numbers · Symbolism · Colour · Order/Sequence · Positive Images · Exaggeration) exceed the [7±2 working-memory](./working-memory.md) ceiling and mix three orthogonal concerns (image transformation, indexing strategy, meta-prerequisite). REMAPS keeps the image-transformation subset, delegates indexing to [peg-audio-visual-matrix](./peg-audio-visual-matrix.md) / [major-system-for-mathematical-notation](./major-system-for-mathematical-notation.md), treats Imagination as a prerequisite trained by [visualization-training](./visualization-training.md), and explicitly rejects Buzan's *Positive Images* constraint — the wiki uses grotesque and disgust imagery freely under the broader Aesthetic move. See [smashin-scope](./smashin-scope.md) for the per-principle mapping and the absorption / reframing / drop decisions.

## Practical Rule

If something is not sticking, do not repeat it unchanged. Change 2-3 dimensions per revision pass — rotate or reverse it, exaggerate it, merge it with an anchor, add play, give it a place, add sensations or symbols. Then test recall again. (source: raw/Neural OS Book/REMAPS.md)

The point is not artistic perfection. The point is retrievability and manipulability. (source: raw/Neural OS Book/REMAPS.md)

## How REMAPS Composes With Other Frameworks

REMAPS sits underneath every encoder; it improves per-slot quality without replacing any of them.

| Encoder | Slots that benefit most from REMAPS |
|---|---|
| [NEDF](./nedf-overview.md) | Name-hook (Modify, Sensations); Distinguisher (Exaggerate); Failure (Move, Play) |
| [CAST](./cast-overview.md) | Node identity (Modify, Sensations); edge verbs (Move); placement (Palace, Path); collision handling (Aesthetic, Symbols) |
| [SPEAR](./spear-overview.md) | Scene (all six moves); failure-mode prompts (Exaggerate, Sensations) |
| [HEART](./heart-overview.md) | Recognition (Sensations); pattern tags (Symbols); treatment cues (Move) |
| [ORACLE](./oracle-overview.md) | Anomaly flavor (Exaggerate); distributional flavor (Aesthetic) |
| [GRACE](./grace-overview.md) | Mostly Sensations and Aesthetic — social-pragmatic scenes are softer than other encoders |
| [Peg matrix](./peg-matrix-remaps-scenes.md) | All 100 cells are REMAPS applied to every audio × visual pair — the canonical worked example of REMAPS at scale |

REMAPS also pairs with [representation-rules](./representation-rules.md): representation-rules tell you *what* a slot needs (concrete first, multi-resolution, etc.); REMAPS tells you *how* to make the filling vivid. Representation-rules are upstream, REMAPS is downstream of "I now have something concrete in this slot but it isn't sticking."

For *rendering* a REMAPS scene as an actual image (gpt-image-2, DALL-E, Midjourney), pair with [CLAMP](./clamp-render-lens.md) — the 5-slot render lens (Camera · Lighting · Aspect/use-mode · Medium · Preserve/Proscribe) that supplies the render directives REMAPS does not name. REMAPS describes the scene; CLAMP describes the capture and constraints. They compose into one prompt template: REMAPS scene sentence → CLAMP M·L·C·A attribute slots → CLAMP P block (invariants + negatives + verbatim text).

## When to Reach for REMAPS

- After encoding, when a recall test fails on a specific card
- During encoding, when an image feels "fine" but bland — bland is a soft warning sign
- Before drilling, when you suspect a scene won't survive mixed interference
- During lifecycle review, when a card's retrieval time is creeping up

## When Not to Use REMAPS

- The first encoding pass — get the slot filled with anything concrete first, then apply REMAPS as a second pass if it doesn't stick. Premature REMAPS adds ceremony.
- Already-fluent material — REMAPS adds noise where retrieval already works.
- Pure structural problems — if the issue is missing anchors or wrong palace placement, REMAPS will not fix it. Reach for [representation-rules](./representation-rules.md) or palace redesign instead.

## External grounding — Dual coding, concrete examples, modality effect

REMAPS implements three of the canonical six learning-science strategies from Dunlosky et al.'s (2013) meta-review:

- **Dual coding** (Paivio 1971; Weinstein/Sumeracki 2018 strategy #5) — REMAPS's Sensations move stacks visual + verbal + motoric channels onto the same encoded artifact. Paivio's finding: redundancy across two channels strengthens retention, *provided the channels are complementary, not duplicative*. REMAPS Sensations is always complementary (sound conveys identity, image conveys structure, motion conveys process) — never the same information twice.
- **Concrete examples** (Weinstein/Sumeracki 2018 strategy #6) — REMAPS's Modify-Merge-Move moves explicitly produce concrete-from-abstract. The code-glyph-remaps-scenes worked-out exercise demonstrates this at scale: 11 of 14 code-glyph shapes needed REMAPS fixes precisely because they were initially too abstract.
- **Modality effect** (Sweller's [Cognitive Load Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_load) — see [representation-rules](./representation-rules.md) External Grounding section) — splitting information across visual + auditory + motoric channels spreads cognitive load across capacity-bounded subsystems instead of overloading one. REMAPS Sensations is the operational implementation.

[memory-palace-for-aphantasia](./memory-palace-for-aphantasia.md) is the constraint-aware reading for users without visual mental imagery: when visual dual-coding is unavailable, REMAPS substitutes motoric + verbal + gaze + spatial channels. The modality effect still applies; the channels just shift.

See [learning-sciences-validation](./learning-sciences-validation.md) for the full mapping of Neural OS onto canonical learning science. REMAPS is the named operational vehicle for three of the six canonical strategies.
