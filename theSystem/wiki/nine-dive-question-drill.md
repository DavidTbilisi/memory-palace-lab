# Nine Dive Question Drill

**Summary**: Sloan's nine starter questions for entering the dive domain — a structured drill that traverses content → process → premise reflection and produces frame-shift, premise-naming, or surfacing of assumptions. The drill: **Why? Why not? Whose frame? Other frames? Missing frames? What else? Where else? When else? How else?** Generally six rounds of these questions are needed before the dive domain delivers — the team must persist past the surface-friendly first three rounds. Sister drill to the [crux-recognition gym](./crux-recognition-gym.md) and to [ORIENT](./orient-method.md), operating at the question-construction layer.

**Sources**:

## The nine questions

| # | Question | Primary reflection type | What it forces |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Why?** | Premise | Reveals the assumed purpose; the most basic premise question |
| 2 | **Why not?** | Premise | Surfaces alternatives that were filtered out; tests rejected options |
| 3 | **Whose frame?** | Premise | Names the perspective the analysis was built from; exposes implicit ownership |
| 4 | **Other frames?** | Premise | Imports other perspectives explicitly; counters [Conformists trap](./six-framing-traps.md) |
| 5 | **Missing frames?** | Premise + Process | Catches the frames *no one* in the room represents; the highest-leverage of the nine |
| 6 | **What else?** | Content + Process | Surfaces data and factors not yet on the table |
| 7 | **Where else?** | Content + Process | Tests spatial / contextual generalization |
| 8 | **When else?** | Content + Process | Tests temporal generalization; surfaces precedents and analogs |
| 9 | **How else?** | Process | Tests method alternatives; the only one focused on procedure |

Six of nine are premise-loaded, by design. The drill is biased toward the rare and high-leverage type per [content-process-premise-reflection](./content-process-premise-reflection.md).

## How to run the drill

### Single-pass form

Take any strategic problem or claim. Run each of the nine questions in order. Write down the surfaced answers (or "none surfaced yet"). The pass typically takes 5-15 minutes.

### Multi-round form (recommended)

Sloan's observation:

> Generally, I find that it tends to take at least six rounds of these questions before executives start to hit on something — that something may be beliefs, assumptions, new creative thoughts, or a new appreciation for the possibility of different frames and assumptions.

So: don't stop at one pass. Run the nine again. And again. Six rounds = 54 questions total. The first three rounds typically surface surface-level answers; rounds 4-6 are where the dive domain delivers.

### Distributed form

Run different questions with different partners. Question 5 ("Missing frames?") asked of an outsider produces different answers than asked of an insider; both are valuable. Reassemble the responses periodically.

## Why six rounds (Sloan's empirical finding)

The first round produces *expected* answers — the team's standing frame surfaces them in 5-10 seconds each. The next two rounds produce more *interesting* answers but still inside the existing frame. **Around round 4** the team begins to exhaust standing answers and is forced into the dive domain — answers slow down, get more uncertain, more affective. **Rounds 5-6** produce premise-naming and frame-shift events.

Stopping at round 1 or 2 is the most common failure. It feels like the drill "didn't produce anything," but actually the drill never got past surf.

## When NOT to use the drill

| Context | Why drill is wrong |
|---|---|
| Operational meeting (per [Surf](./surf-dive-learning-domains.md) mode) | Drill is overhead; team needs decision velocity |
| Live crisis | Crisis demands convergent action; dive comes later in the [AAR](./after-action-review.md) |
| Single-skill drill session ([Earth-element](./automaticity-and-reflex-training.md) reps) | Drill disrupts automation; force-Cognitive only at the [crux](./crux-move.md) |
| Dialogue with someone in shutdown | If a participant is in grip / inferior-function takeover, dive questions feel like attacks; resolve state first |

## METER

- `drill_rounds_completed` — count per dialogue; pass ≥3, target ≥6
- `dive_hit_round` — which round produced the first premise-shift; track distribution; lower is better but very-low (round 1) is suspicious
- `nine_question_coverage` — fraction of the 9 actually asked per round; pass ≥7/9
- `missing_frame_named` — boolean; the most valuable single output of the drill; pass ≥1 per session
- `drill_chickened_out_count` — incidents of stopping at round 1-2; pass ≤1/week

## Drill ladder (sister format to dsa-tier1-drill-ladder)

| Stage | Drill | Pass floor |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Orientation** | Memorize the 9 questions in order | Recite all 9 from cold in ≤15s |
| **1 — Lamp** | Run the 9 questions on a *low-stakes* topic of your choosing | Complete 1 full pass in ≤10 min |
| **2 — Scale** | Run the 9 on a real strategic problem; record surfaced answers | 1 full pass in ≤15 min; ≥3 non-trivial answers |
| **3 — Sword** | Run 6 rounds on a high-stakes problem; partner-checked | 6 rounds in ≤60 min; ≥1 missing-frame named; ≥1 premise-shift |
| **4 — Distribution** | Run different questions with different partners; reassemble | 3+ partners; reassembled within 1 week |
| **5 — Recursive** | After the drill, run the drill *on the drill* — "what else could we have asked?" | Surface ≥1 new question not in the 9 |
| **6 — Internalized** | Drill fires unprompted in everyday strategic conversations | METER detects ≥1 unprompted fire/day |
| **7 — Zenith** | You routinely identify when *not* to drill (see "When NOT to use" above) | <1 misfire/quarter |

## Failure modes

1. **Round-1-stopping** — most common; team stops after the first pass with shallow answers. Defense: explicit minimum-round count before the meeting.
2. **Surface-friendly answers** — team gives answers that satisfy the question's letter without engaging the spirit. Defense: ask "is this answer in the dive domain or did we surf it?"
3. **Drill-as-attack** — using the 9 questions to challenge a specific person rather than the problem. Defense: ask the questions in the third person ("what would a stranger think we're missing?").
4. **Drill-as-checklist** — going through the 9 mechanically without actual engagement. Defense: pause between questions; if no twitch (per [content-process-premise-reflection](./content-process-premise-reflection.md) §The twitch), the question didn't land.
5. **Drill in the wrong mode** — running it in surf-only mode produces only content answers. Defense: explicitly load divergent cluster before starting (see [strategic-thinking-cognitive-cluster](./strategic-thinking-cognitive-cluster.md)).

## Mnemonic

A Velvet Aeon Cosmic-mode **circle of nine standing stones**, each engraved with one of the questions in luminous script. A **woman in a hooded robe** walks the perimeter, **touching each stone in turn**. At each stone she pauses, **the stone glows softly when her question lands**, the answer drifts up as **luminous mist**. She is **fragile/luminous** in the face. The first three stones glow easily, with bright mist. The next three glow more dimly, mist thinner. **The seventh, eighth, and ninth stones** are dark — she must complete the circle to reach them, then begin again. **Six complete circuits** must be walked before the *centermost* stone (not part of the nine; representing the surfaced premise) ignites with a steady flame.

The woman's **paintbrush** in her belt is the [metaphor + drawing trio](./and-polarity-thinking.md) tool — once the centermost stone lights, she sketches the premise as a metaphor. **Above** the circle, the sky is divided by a hard horizon: **surf clouds** above (questions in surface mode), **dive ocean** below (where the answers come from). Long hair lifted in the standing-stone wind. Single light source: a single low moon to the east.
