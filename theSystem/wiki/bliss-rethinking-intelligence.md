# Rethinking Intelligence (Bliss)

**Summary**: *Rethinking Intelligence: A Radical New Understanding of Our Human Potential* (Rina Bliss, Harper Wave 2023, ~250 pp) is the wiki's source for the **biological case against fixed intelligence** — the argument that measured cognitive performance is primarily environment-determined, and that the machinery through which environment acts is the [epigenome](./epigenetics-and-intelligence.md) rather than the genome. Bliss is a sociologist of science (Rutgers; Ph.D. Columbia), and the book's two halves are graded differently by this wiki: **chapters 1–7 carry the load-bearing science**, while **chapters 8–9 are sociological advocacy** and are flagged as Bliss's position rather than cited as findings. This page is the source-summary / routing owner; the four concepts it contributed are defined on their own pages.

## What the book argues

One thesis in two moves. First, **the measurement is not what it claims to be**: IQ testing originates in Galton's eugenics programme (1886), not in value-neutral science, and the history is not incidental to the instrument — it shaped what the test was built to detect. Second, **the biology does not support fixedness**: genome-wide association studies explain only about **12% of the variance in educational attainment** even at a sample size of ~3 million (Lee et al. 2018), which leaves the overwhelming majority of variation to environment and to gene-*regulation* rather than gene-*content*.

The constructive half is the mechanism Bliss puts in that gap, which [epigenetics-and-intelligence](./epigenetics-and-intelligence.md) owns: regulation of gene *expression* rather than gene *content*. The book's practical claim follows from it: if regulation rather than sequence is doing the work, then experience is not merely correlated with cognitive outcomes, it is *causally upstream* of them through a documented biochemical route.

## The seven load-bearing claims (Chs 1–7)

| # | Claim | Evidence as recorded | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | IQ originates with eugenics, not neutral science | Galton 1886 | [iq-history-and-critique](./iq-history-and-critique.md) |
| 2 | Genetics explains only ~12% of educational-attainment variance | Lee et al. 2018 GWAS, ~3M participants | [iq-history-and-critique](./iq-history-and-critique.md) |
| 3 | Regulation above the sequence, not the sequence itself, is where experience acts | methylation → silencing; histone modification → inaccessibility | [epigenetics-and-intelligence](./epigenetics-and-intelligence.md) |
| 4 | **Learning novelty triggers BDNF** — novel synaptic demand → demethylation → BDNF expression | added to the wiki as the **6th lever** on the BDNF page | [bdnf-and-neurogenesis](./bdnf-and-neurogenesis.md) |
| 5 | Epigenetic marks can be inherited across generations | Dutch Hunger Winter 1944; Holocaust-survivor offspring; POW studies | [epigenetics-and-intelligence](./epigenetics-and-intelligence.md) |
| 6 | Chronic social stress causes cumulative biological damage | Geronimus 1992; accelerated cortical thinning per Harvard Aging Brain Study + Human Connectome Project | [social-weathering](./social-weathering.md) |
| 7 | Population-level score movement rules out a primarily-genetic account | the Flynn Effect — ~3 points/decade globally; Black Americans outpacing the mean post-civil-rights | [iq-history-and-critique](./iq-history-and-critique.md) |

Claim 7 is the argument's keystone and worth stating plainly: scores have risen far faster than any genetic change could account for, and they rose fastest in exactly the populations whose environments improved most. A trait that moves that quickly, in that pattern, is not primarily heritable in the everyday sense of the word.

## What it contributed to Neural OS

Two things, and only one of them is a page.

**The 6th BDNF lever is the operational payoff.** Before this ingest, [bdnf-and-neurogenesis](./bdnf-and-neurogenesis.md) listed five lifestyle levers for raising BDNF — aerobic exercise, sleep, intermittent fasting, stress reduction, sunlight. Bliss supplies a sixth that is *learning itself*: novel synaptic demand drives demethylation and BDNF expression. That closes a loop the rest of the wiki was already assuming — the substrate that makes learning possible is itself raised by learning, provided the material is **novel** rather than merely repeated. It is the biological argument for why a drill ladder must keep escalating, and it is registered as a convergence unlock with [ok-plateau](./ok-plateau.md) in [composability-index](./composability-index.md).

**[connected-learning](./connected-learning.md) is the pedagogical mode** the book argues is most aligned with the brain's default state — social, personal-interest-driven, civic, and digital. The wiki holds it as a mode description, not a prescription.

## Reading discipline: where the science stops

The ingest recorded an explicit boundary and this page keeps it. **Chapters 8–9 are sociological advocacy** — policy and social argument built on top of the science, flagged throughout the wiki's pages as *Bliss's sociological position* rather than as findings. The distinction is not a dismissal: it is the same means/ends split [truth-vs-effectiveness](./truth-vs-effectiveness.md) owns. Chapters 1–7 make claims about mechanism, where evidence has jurisdiction; chapters 8–9 make claims about what should follow, where it does not.

A second calibration runs the other way. Bliss's anti-hereditarian conclusion is *stronger* than the narrower claim the wiki actually files: the wiki takes from her that **measured performance is highly environment-determined and epigenetically mediated**, which is well supported. It does not file the further claim that intelligence is *wholly* socially constructed — that belongs to the advocacy half.

## Mnemonic

**SEED** — a seed's outcome is set by soil, not only by the seed: **S**ocial weathering (stress is biological damage) · **E**pigenome (the regulatory layer that reads the environment) · **E**nvironment wins (Flynn Effect, ~3 pts/decade) · **D**NA explains little (~12% of variance). The genome is the seed; everything interesting is the soil.
