# Automatic Success Mechanism (ASM)

**Summary**: The cybernetic servomechanism inside the human brain and nervous system that takes a target from the [self-image](./self-image.md) and zig-zags toward it via negative feedback. Maltz's reframing of the "subconscious" as a goal-seeking machine — same architecture as a guided missile or autopilot. Operated by the five-letter cycle **AIM · TRUST · RELAX · LEARN · DO**. The ASM is what *executes* the action the self-image authorises; it is downstream of the self-image but upstream of conscious motor planning. This is the wiki's owner page for the concept; canonical source is [psycho-cybernetics-maltz](./psycho-cybernetics-maltz.md) Ch 2.

## Definition

The ASM is the servomechanism formed by the brain + nervous system, conceived as a goal-seeking machine that:

1. Receives a target from the [self-image](./self-image.md) via [imagination](./theater-of-the-mind.md)
2. Initiates motor / cognitive activity toward the target
3. Receives feedback from the senses ("sense organs" in Maltz / Wiener's language)
4. Self-corrects via negative feedback when off-course
5. Stores the successful response for future use as a habit

(source: pscyho-cybernetics-book-maxwell-maltz.pdf Ch 2, "Your Built-In Guidance System")

> "Your servo-mechanism is capable of being an Automatic Success Mechanism or an Automatic Failure Mechanism. That depends on what marching orders or programming it gets through your self-image." (source: pscyho-cybernetics-book-maxwell-maltz.pdf Ch 2)

The same machine runs both modes — the difference is the work-order. With a success-typed self-image, it runs as ASM; with a failure-typed self-image, it runs as the Automatic Failure Mechanism (see [failure-mechanism](./failure-mechanism.md)).

## The cybernetic lineage

The word "cybernetics" comes from the Greek *kybernētēs*, "steersman." Norbert Wiener coined the modern field in 1948 — the study of control and communication in animals, humans, and machines. Maltz applied Wiener's principles to the human nervous system: the same negative-feedback architecture that lets a guided missile correct its course mid-flight lets you pick up a pencil from a table without consciously computing muscle activations.

(source: pscyho-cybernetics-book-maxwell-maltz.pdf Ch 2, citing Wiener and von Neumann)

This is the **External canon citation** registered in [glossary](./glossary.md) §External canon citations: Cybernetics (Wiener 1948) and Psycho-Cybernetics (Maltz 1960) as the originator pair.

## Two ASM modes (Maltz's typology)

| Mode | Target | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Type 1** — target known, objective is to reach it | Pencil on the table; basket on the court; sentence to finish; sale to close | Guided missile, autopilot, conscious goal-pursuit |
| **Type 2** — target unknown, objective is to discover or locate it | A forgotten name; an answer to a problem; a creative idea; a new theorem | Search-engine-in-the-mind; incubation; the famous overnight-solution pattern |

The brain runs both modes. Type 1 is what gets drilled in the [red-queen-skill-gym](./red-queen-skill-gym.md); Type 2 is what gets primed during sleep, walks, or relaxed states.

(source: pscyho-cybernetics-book-maxwell-maltz.pdf Ch 2, "Understanding the Two General Types of Servo-Mechanisms")

## The five-letter operating cycle — AIM · TRUST · RELAX · LEARN · DO

(source: pscyho-cybernetics-book-maxwell-maltz.pdf Ch 2, Mental Training Exercise — "Memorize the following basic principles…")

Maltz's own 5-step cycle for running the ASM. The wiki adopts this verbatim — the words *are* the mnemonic.

### AIM

> "Your built-in success mechanism must have a goal or 'target.' This goal, or target, must be conceived of as 'already in existence now,' either in actual or potential form." (source: pscyho-cybernetics-book-maxwell-maltz.pdf Ch 2)

The aim is not "I want to be X" (future-tense, deficit-implied). It is "X already exists; my work is to recognise / reach / serve it." This is the same target-conception used in Maltz's [Theater of the Mind](./theater-of-the-mind.md) — the rehearsed scene is treated as *current*, not *aspirational*.

**Wiki cross-reference**: Great Work *Conjunction* (Select the rule/pattern that applies); [problem-solving-os](./problem-solving-os.md) step 2 (Classify and frame).

### TRUST

> "The automatic mechanism is tele-logical; that is, it operates on, or must be oriented to, 'end results,' goals. Do not be discouraged because the means may not be apparent. It is the function of the automatic mechanism to supply the means when you supply the goal." (source: pscyho-cybernetics-book-maxwell-maltz.pdf Ch 2)

The conscious mind picks the *end*; the ASM supplies the *means*. Premature mean-engineering by the conscious mind (worry, over-planning, micromanaging the steps) clamps the mechanism and prevents the negative-feedback correction from running. Trust is operational, not metaphysical: it's the discipline of *not* clamping.

**Wiki cross-reference**: Great Work *Distillation* (Feedback); [BRIDGE LOAD](./bridge-load.md) guard slot — the analogy survives because we trust the source-domain structure does the work, we don't reconstruct it.

### RELAX

> "Do not be afraid of making mistakes or of temporary failures. All servo-mechanisms achieve a goal by negative feedback, or by going forward, making mistakes, and immediately correcting course." (source: pscyho-cybernetics-book-maxwell-maltz.pdf Ch 2)

Errors are not failures — they are the *medium* through which the ASM steers. A guided missile that never deviated would never need correction; it would also never have a target. Tension and fear of error clamp the system; relaxation lets the corrective loop run.

**Wiki cross-reference**: [PULSE](./pulse-overview.md) low-stress gate — under high Stress (S≥4), the ASM cannot run the corrective loop; relaxation must be restored first. [F·A·I·L·U·R·E.](./failure-mechanism.md) scan triggers when this gate fails.

### LEARN

> "Skill learning of any kind is accomplished by trial and error, mentally correcting your aim after an error, until you achieve a 'successful' motion, movement, or performance. After that, further learning and continued success are accomplished by forgetting the past errors, and remembering the successful response, so that it can be 'imitated.'" (source: pscyho-cybernetics-book-maxwell-maltz.pdf Ch 2)

The ASM **remembers successes and forgets failures**. This is operationally backwards from the wiki's default error-log discipline, which *retains* failures for mining. The reconciliation: the **conscious** layer retains failures for diagnostic mining ([active-recall](./active-recall.md) error logs); the **unconscious / motor** layer stores only the successful response pattern for imitation. The two layers serve different purposes and should not be confused.

**Wiki cross-reference**: Great Work *Fermentation* (Execute); [active-recall](./active-recall.md) testing-effect; [automaticity-and-reflex-training](./automaticity-and-reflex-training.md) reflex-card success-test.

### DO

> "You must learn to trust your creative mechanism to do its work and not 'jam it' by becoming too concerned or too anxious as to whether it will work or not, or by attempting to force it by too much conscious effort. You must let it work, rather than make it work… You must not wait to act until you have proof. You must act as if it is there, and it will come through. 'Do the thing and you will have the power,' said Emerson." (source: pscyho-cybernetics-book-maxwell-maltz.pdf Ch 2, citing Emerson)

**Act as if** — the most-cited Maltz operational instruction. Behave as the person you've rehearsed in [theater-of-the-mind](./theater-of-the-mind.md), not as the person the current self-image still describes. The acting *generates* the data the self-image needs to update.

**Wiki cross-reference**: Great Work *Coagulation* (Automate); [automaticity-and-reflex-training](./automaticity-and-reflex-training.md) reflex-card execution; [spark-overview](./spark-overview.md) T2/T3 ceremonies which mark the moments where "acting as if" became "actually being."

## How the ASM finds answers (Type 2 mode)

When the target is *unknown* (an answer, a creative idea, a forgotten name), the ASM operates as a search engine over stored memories. The conscious mind poses the problem and then releases it; the ASM scans associatively and surfaces the answer when found.

(source: pscyho-cybernetics-book-maxwell-maltz.pdf Ch 2, "How Your Brain Finds Answers to Problems")

**Wiki cross-reference**: this is the *incubation effect* documented by Wallas 1926 (*The Art of Thought* — preparation → incubation → illumination → verification). Modern replication: Sio & Ormerod 2009 meta-analysis confirms incubation produces small but robust effects on creative problem-solving.

Operational protocol:

1. Saturate consciously — gather facts, frame the question, attempt directly (preparation)
2. Release — sleep, walk, shower, do something unrelated (incubation)
3. Capture — keep pen and paper nearby for the moment the answer surfaces (illumination)
4. Verify — test the surfaced answer against constraints (verification)

Maltz cites Louis Agassiz's fossil-fish dream as the canonical case. He attributes some of this capacity to "universal mind" / telepathy — see [psycho-cybernetics-maltz](./psycho-cybernetics-maltz.md) §"What Maltz claims that needs caveats" for the wiki's framing.

## When the ASM mis-fires

The mechanism is **impersonal** — it executes whatever target it receives. A negative self-image produces an Automatic Failure Mechanism that pursues failure as faithfully as the ASM pursues success. The mis-fire is *never* in the machine; it's always in the target (the self-image) feeding it.

Warning signs that the failure-mode is active: see [F·A·I·L·U·R·E. signals](./failure-mechanism.md) (Frustration · Aggressiveness · Insecurity · Loneliness · Uncertainty · Resentment · Emptiness).

---

## Failure modes

1. **Skipping AIM** — drilling without a clearly conceived target. The mechanism has nothing to steer toward. Pass-floor: target written in present-tense, "already exists" framing.
2. **Skipping TRUST** — conscious over-planning of the means clamps the corrective loop. Detection: hours spent reorganising the approach without taking a single action toward the target.
3. **Skipping RELAX** — fear of error prevents the error → correction → success loop. Detection: high Stress, low movement.
4. **Skipping LEARN** — repeating the same error without registering the correction. Detection: same gym-pass-floor failure pattern across multiple sessions without delta.
5. **Skipping DO** — waiting for proof before acting. The proof comes *from* acting. Detection: long analysis-paralysis windows; "I'll do it once I'm ready" without movement.
6. **Mistaking the conscious error-log for the unconscious learning store** — see §LEARN. The two stores serve different purposes; conflating them produces either neurotic failure-rehearsal or sloppy diagnosis.
