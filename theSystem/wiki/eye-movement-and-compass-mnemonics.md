# Eye Movement And Compass Mnemonics

**Summary**: A synthesis note connecting eye movement psychology to compass-style mnemonics. The central idea is that deliberate gaze shifts can reinforce directional anchors, quadrant retrieval, and angle navigation by turning spatial memory into a small motor routine.

## What This Page Is

This is a **synthesis layer**, not a direct extract from one raw source.

The existing trig pages already treat recall as navigation on a compass. This page makes one additional inference:

- if the mnemonic is spatial
- and retrieval happens by directional placement
- then **eye movement can become part of the retrieval interface**

That fits the broader Neural OS diagnosis that embodied and sensorimotor encoding are still underweighted. (source: trigonometry-compass-palace.md; unit-circle-as-compass.md; missing-encoding-layers.md; raw/Index - Neural OS.md)

## Core Claim

Deliberate gaze shifts can help because they bind three things together:

- **attention**
- **direction**
- **motor pattern**

Instead of remembering an angle as a detached symbol, you can:

1. center your gaze
2. move your eyes toward the correct compass direction
3. let the direction trigger angle, sign, and coordinate recall

This turns the compass mnemonic into a small embodied retrieval loop.

## Compass Mapping Through Gaze

The most useful correspondence is:

| Eye movement | Compass meaning | Trig meaning |
|---|---|---|
| look right | East | positive x, cosine positive |
| look left | West | negative x, cosine negative |
| look up | North | positive y, sine positive |
| look down | South | negative y, sine negative |
| look up-right | NE | Q1 |
| look up-left | NW | Q2 |
| look down-left | SW | Q3 |
| look down-right | SE | Q4 |

This is the simplest bridge between eye movement and the existing compass topology. It preserves the same geometry already used in the trig pages. (source: unit-circle-as-compass.md; quadrant-sign-patterns.md)

## Why This Helps

The working psychological idea is modest:

- gaze direction organizes attention spatially
- repeated scan paths become familiar retrieval routines
- motor involvement can strengthen weak abstract recall

In Neural OS terms, this adds a small **sensorimotor layer** to a mnemonic that is already strong spatially.

The result is not new math. It is a stronger access path to the same math.

## Retrieval Rules

### 1. Center = Reset

Start from center fixation.

That acts as:

- origin
- neutral state
- reset point before choosing direction

### 2. Horizontal Gaze = Cosine Axis

If the question is mainly about cosine sign or x-position:

- move gaze right for positive
- move gaze left for negative

This reinforces the rule:

`x = cos theta`

### 3. Vertical Gaze = Sine Axis

If the question is mainly about sine sign or y-position:

- move gaze up for positive
- move gaze down for negative

This reinforces the rule:

`y = sin theta`

### 4. Diagonal Gaze = Quadrant Selection

If the question is "which quadrant is this angle in?":

- snap gaze to the diagonal first
- then recover both signs from that quadrant

This is often faster than searching for a memorized sign table.

### 5. Circular Sweep = Angle Travel

If the question is about angle progression, coterminal movement, or "where do I land?":

- trace the turn with your eyes around the compass

This makes the unit circle feel like motion instead of a static diagram.

## Practical Drill

Use this short retrieval drill:

1. fix eyes at center
2. hear or read an angle
3. snap gaze to the matching compass direction
4. say the quadrant
5. say the sign of cosine from left/right position
6. say the sign of sine from up/down position

Compressed loop:

`center -> direction -> quadrant -> cos/sin sign`

That is the eye-movement version of the existing compass mnemonic.

## Good Use Cases

- unit circle angle orientation
- quadrant sign recall
- cosine vs sine polarity
- teaching beginners who need a more embodied cue
- fast mental checking under light pressure

## Limits

This layer helps most when the problem is:

- directional confusion
- symbolic overload
- weak quadrant intuition

It helps less when the problem is:

- missing conceptual understanding
- missing special-angle values
- algebraic trig identities
- proof-based derivation

So eye movement is a **supporting encoder**, not a replacement for the existing trig structure.

## Bottom Line

Compass mnemonics already make trig spatial.

Adding deliberate eye movement makes them:

- spatial
- attentional
- lightly embodied

That is a cleaner psychology connection than treating the mnemonic as visual-only.
