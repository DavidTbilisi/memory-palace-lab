# Mnemonics

**Summary**: The concept page the wiki's whole mnemonic toolkit is instances of — the deliberate re-encoding of material into a form memory retrieves well, traded against the time that encoding costs. Its load-bearing claim is a boundary rather than a technique: **a mnemonic does not make material easier to understand, it makes it easier to *find*.** It buys retrieval and pays in encoding time, which makes the decision to build one economic — worth it only when the encoding cost is less than the recall it buys over the horizon the material is needed for. That single sentence is what turns "use mnemonics" from advice into a rule with a failure condition, and it is why [mnemonic-methods-master](./mnemonic-methods-master.md)'s tier discipline exists at all. Sibling to that page and distinguished from it by the question each answers: this page answers *what a mnemonic is and why it works*; the master answers *which one to reach for*.

## Why this page exists

The wiki had 269 pages in the mnemonics cluster and no page that said what a mnemonic *is*. Every layer below this one was owned — the encoders own their formats, [memory-palace](./memory-palace.md) and [peg-system](./peg-system.md) own the two anchor families, [mnemonic-methods-master](./mnemonic-methods-master.md) owns the routing — but the term at the top of all of them was named without a page behind it, exactly the shape that surfaced [peg-system](./peg-system.md) the day before.

The split from the master page is the whole justification for a separate file. [mnemonic-methods-master](./mnemonic-methods-master.md) states its own boundary plainly: *"Where other pages explain how a method works, this page explains whether to use it at all."* That leaves a third question unowned — **why any of it works** — and the answer is not a preamble to method selection. It is the thing that decides when *not* to encode, which no ranking table can express.

## What a mnemonic is

A mnemonic is a deliberate re-encoding: material arrives in a form memory handles badly (arbitrary, unordered, abstract, undifferentiated) and is rewritten into a form memory handles well (spatial, vivid, ordered, distinctive) with a **binding** back to the original.

Three parts are constitutive, and dropping any one produces something that is not a mnemonic:

1. **A structure that already exists** — a route, an index, a body map, a known song. It must pre-date the material, or the encoding cost includes building it.
2. **A transformation** that turns the material into something that structure can hold. [remaps](./remaps.md) owns the move catalogue.
3. **A binding rule** that makes the transformed image recover the original, not merely resemble it.

Part 3 is where amateur mnemonics fail. An image that is vivid but does not *decode* is a memory of an image. The encoders each specify their binding — this is what a NEDF name-hook or a CAST edge label is for — and the requirement is the same across all of them: recall of the image must reconstruct the material, not just prove the image was stored.

## The four mechanisms

Everything in the toolkit exploits some combination of four. They are not alternatives; a good encoding stacks them.

| Mechanism | What it exploits | Where the wiki uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **[Elaboration](./elaboration.md)** | Retrieval improves with the number of routes to an item, and an encoding adds routes the material did not have | Every encoder; [nedf-overview](./nedf-overview.md)'s four slots are four routes to one concept |
| **Distinctiveness** | An item unlike its neighbours is recalled at primacy/recency rates from the middle of a list — the Von Restorff effect, owned by [serial-position-curve](./serial-position-curve.md) | The "make it bizarre" instruction in every image-based method |
| **Dual coding** | Image and word are separately stored and separately retrievable, so encoding both doubles the chance one survives | [memory-palace](./memory-palace.md) loci; [peg-audio-visual-matrix](./peg-audio-visual-matrix.md)'s paired channels |
| **Borrowed order** | Sequence is expensive to store and cheap to *reuse*; a route or an index supplies order the material lacked | [memory-palace](./memory-palace.md) (order in the route) · [peg-system](./peg-system.md) (order in the index) |

The fourth is the one most often missed. Ordered material is not remembered by storing its order; it is remembered by hanging it on order that was already there. That is why both anchor families install their structure *before* any material arrives, and why [peg-system](./peg-system.md) describes the effort as inverted rather than reduced.

## The law: encoding cost against recall bought

A mnemonic is an investment with a known cost and an uncertain return. The cost is paid immediately, in attention, at the moment you least want to spend it. The return arrives later and only if the material is actually needed again.

**Build one when the encoding cost is less than the recall it buys over the horizon the material is needed for.** Three corollaries follow, and each is visible in the existing pages:

- **The tier discipline is this law, applied.** [mnemonic-methods-master](./mnemonic-methods-master.md)'s rule that Tier 3 waits for Tier 1 fluency is not aesthetic conservatism. Embodied encodings have a high fixed cost and a narrow return; concept encodings have a low cost and a broad one. *"Beautiful gestures don't compensate for absent NEDF cards"* is the law stated in the negative.
- **Short horizons do not justify encoding.** Material needed once, this afternoon, is cheaper looked up than encoded. Mnemonics are for what must be available without the source present.
- **An encoding is a liability until it is maintained.** The cost is not one-off. [spaced-repetition](./spaced-repetition.md) and [active-recall](./active-recall.md) are what convert a built encoding into a retained one, and an unmaintained palace decays into a memory of having built a palace.

## What a mnemonic is not

Three exclusions, ruled out deliberately, because each is a live confusion the wiki has had to correct elsewhere.

- **Not comprehension.** A handle can only be attached to something with a shape. Encoding material you do not understand stores the words and loses the meaning, and the loss is silent — recall succeeds while understanding is absent. productive-struggle and [memory-paradox](./memory-paradox.md) own the reasons the effortful path is the one that leaves understanding behind.
- **Not practice.** A mnemonic makes a fact retrievable; it does not make a skill fluent. The gap between "can recall the rule" and "applies the rule under time pressure" is owned by [automaticity-and-reflex-training](./automaticity-and-reflex-training.md) and measured by [skill-progression-stages](./skill-progression-stages.md). Encoding a procedure and drilling it are different acts with different costs, and mistaking the first for the second is one route into the [ok-plateau](./ok-plateau.md).
- **Not storage.** The image is a retrieval handle, not the item. This is why [memory-reconsolidation](./memory-reconsolidation.md) matters: the material is re-written each time it is recalled, and a handle that is never pulled does not preserve what it points at.
