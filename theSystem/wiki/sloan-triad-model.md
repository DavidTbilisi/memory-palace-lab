# Sloan Triad Model of Strategy

**Summary**: Julia Sloan's three-dot operating model of strategy: **Strategic Thinking · Strategic Planning · Strategic Implementation**, connected by imaginary elastic bands. Not a cycle. Not a sequence. A dynamic, fragmented, oscillating focus that surfaces one dot at a time depending on business need while the other two recede. Each dot is supported by a **distinctly different cognitive cluster** — the operating insight of the model and the basis for [the cluster page](./strategic-thinking-cognitive-cluster.md).

**Sources**:
- [sloan-learning-to-think-strategically](./sloan-learning-to-think-strategically.md) — source summary
- Sloan, *Learning to Think Strategically* (4th ed., 2020), Ch 4 §"The Sloan Triad Model of Strategy™", Figures 4.2 and 4.3

## The three dots

| Dot | Intent | Cognitive cluster | Typical learning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strategic Thinking** | *Suspend* problem-solving — diverge the problem; deep-dive into the underlying premise; identify the *real* and *right* problem | Divergent · a-rational · conceptual · panoramic · intuitive · metaphoric · symbolic · critical-reflective | Informal — experience, dialogue, the arts |
| **Strategic Planning** | *Solve* the problem — produce a "product" (the plan) | Convergent · concrete · linear · analytical · rational · reductionist · literal · objective · logical | Formal — courses, models, schools, frameworks |
| **Strategic Implementation** | *Do it* — execute the plan in alignment with the strategic intent | Same convergent/rational cluster as planning, plus execution discipline | Formal — operational training, project management |

**Connections**: imaginary elastic bands. The dots are inseparable but not sequenced. A successful strategy requires all three; missing one collapses the strategy. The bands stretch and contract as the business focus moves.

## The "in operation" picture (Figure 4.3)

At any moment **one dot is at the forefront**; the other two **recede**. The leader's job: state explicitly which dot is in focus, and signal the cluster shift. Sloan's wording:

> Leaders need to develop the habit of pressing a metaphorical "pause button" at any point in the strategy process to advance the appropriate domain to the forefront; sometimes for a brief meeting and other times for a period of several months.

**The duration on any dot is variable** — it depends on the business need, not on a calendar. "When the pear is ripe, it falls from the tree" (Sloan's favorite Indian proverb on this point).

## Two common misconceptions

1. **The triad is a cycle.** It is not. There is no fixed order Thinking → Planning → Implementation. The order is determined by business need, and revisits are normal.
2. **The three dots are equal-time, equal-effort.** They are not. A given strategy might spend 80% on Thinking, 15% on Planning, 5% on Implementation — or the reverse. The split is per-situation.

## Why the cluster shift matters

The cluster for Thinking can **actively undermine** the cluster for Planning, and vice versa. Specifically:

- Bringing the **convergent/reductionist** cluster into a strategic-thinking session collapses the divergent process prematurely — the team "solves" a wrongly-framed problem.
- Bringing the **divergent/a-rational** cluster into a strategic-planning session leaves the plan vague, unmeasurable, and unimplementable.

This is why explicit signaling matters. The Swedish CEO Sloan quotes:

> The way I think when I am in this place is very different from the other. This idea that strategy is linear or that it follows a flow is false. It is not. It is risky and messy. For me, it is helpful to know which is my focus at the time.

## Self-diagnostic — which dot am I in?

| Question | If yes |
|---|---|
| Am I suspending solutions and asking "is this even the right problem?" | Thinking |
| Am I converging on a single answer with metrics and a timeline? | Planning |
| Am I executing a plan against a delineated strategic intent? | Implementation |
| Am I tense because I want all three at once? | You're in *no dot* — the elastic is overstretched |

## Common over-reliance failure modes

| Pattern | Failure |
|---|---|
| **Thinking-only** | Endless exploration, no plan, no shipping; the team is "strategic" but produces nothing |
| **Planning-only** | Detailed plans for the wrong problem; "100% on schedule for the wrong destination" |
| **Implementation-only** | Frantic execution of inherited assumptions; market overtakes the team |

The wiki adds a fourth: **Cluster-confused** — leader thinks they are on the Thinking dot but the cognitive cluster they apply is convergent. Symptom: meetings that produce decisions instead of questions.

## Position in the wiki

The triad sits at the **strategy-meta layer** above the [problem-solving-os](./problem-solving-os.md) type-classifier. The classifier picks a problem type; the triad picks a *strategy mode* — that is, which cluster the team should run. The two are orthogonal: any problem type can be approached from any of the three dots, but the cluster shift is mandatory.

## Mnemonic

A craftsperson's bench in a Velvet Aeon Mortal-mode workshop. Three tools are laid out: **a brass lantern with refracted light** (Thinking — the lamp scatters into every corner of the room, illuminating shapes the worker hadn't named), **a steel compass and ruler in tight precision** (Planning — converging to a single inked point), **a heavy hammer mid-strike** (Implementation — singular force, no negotiation). Each tool is connected to the others by a **black elastic band** stretched taut between handles; touching any tool tightens the other two. The workshop window shows **an unfinished horizon** — the work is never done. The craftsperson's hand hovers over the lantern (Thinking is in focus); the compass and hammer are softly receded into deeper register.

## Memory checksum

- **3** dots (Thinking · Planning · Implementation)
- **2** cognitive clusters (divergent/a-rational + convergent/analytical) — Implementation reuses Planning's cluster
- **3** bands (elastic, not arrows; non-sequenced)
- **1** dot in focus at a time
- **4** failure modes (Thinking-only · Planning-only · Implementation-only · Cluster-confused)
- **1** explicit-signaling rule ("state which dot you're on at the start of the meeting")

If you can recite **3-2-3-1-4-1** within 30 seconds and *draw the triad with elastic bands instead of arrows*, the model is encoded.
