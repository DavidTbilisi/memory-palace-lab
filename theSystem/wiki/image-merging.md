# Image Merging — One Percept Per Edge

**Summary**: The strongest edge-encoding upgrade in [CAST](./cast-overview.md): collapse source animal + relation + target animal into **one composite image** in which no element can be removed without destroying the scene. Three potential points of failure become one retrievable percept — and the scene's own coherence becomes its integrity check.

**Sources**:

## The rule

> Do not place the elements near each other. **Merge them so that one cannot exist without the other.** (source: CAST and Georgian Node System.md)

A node-edge-node sequence stored as three separate images — source animal, scene, target animal — has three potential points of failure, held together by effort. Merged, it is one image retrieved as one percept. The raw source calls this "the single largest quality jump in the whole system." (source: CAST and Georgian Node System.md)

**Before**: *Eagle (source) → Giant flowing cloud in a red cave → Owl (target)* — three images.

**After**: *Eagle flies through the Owl's chest carrying a glowing cloud — permanently lodged there, the cave walls closing around both of them* — one image carrying source, relationship type, stream content, time quality, and target. (source: CAST and Georgian Node System.md)

## How to merge (Tier 2 edges)

1. Start with the two animals **facing each other**
2. The **Action determines the physical contact**: crushing = one animal pinning the other · flowing = one pouring something into the other · spreading = one exhaling onto the other · exploding = one detonating inside the other
3. The **Stream becomes the object passing between them**
4. The **Time environment wraps the whole scene** (cave walls, ocean floor, storm clouds, open sky)
5. **Exaggerate until neither animal could be removed** without destroying the image

(source: CAST and Georgian Node System.md)

For Tier 1 edges the merge is simpler: the source animal performs the verb *physically on* the target animal. Eagle pierces Owl with its beak. Owl nests inside Pig. One image, zero ambiguity. (source: CAST and Georgian Node System.md)

## Beyond edges — merging an item's attributes

The rule generalizes past [CAST](./cast-overview.md) edges. Any time one item carries several things that must come back together — a source, a relation and a target here; an item's category, condition and era in [multi-attribute-encoding](./multi-attribute-encoding.md) — the choice is the same: fuse them so no element is removable, or leave them adjacent and accept one independent failure point per element. The multi-attribute case makes the cost explicit: stacked attributes fail *silently*, because the surviving elements still make the item feel recalled.

## Incoherence is the checksum

If any element is missing from a properly merged scene, the image becomes incoherent — animals no longer interacting, a stream with nowhere to go. That incoherence is a built-in integrity signal: this is Level 1 (scene integrity) of the four-level [mnemonic-checksum](./mnemonic-checksum.md). (source: CAST and Georgian Node System.md)

The test for a finished merge: **can you remove one element without the image collapsing?** If yes, keep merging.

## Worked disambiguation — two colliding edges

From the city-streets example, the two most confusable edges (both Central → South):

- **River Lane merged**: *Eagle's talons grip a pitcher, water streams from it directly into the Dinosaur's open mouth, the Dinosaur half-submerged and drinking* — one image, inseparable.
- **Old Road merged**: *Eagle's wings push a boulder that rolls into the Dinosaur's ribcage, dust and rock filling the air around both* — one image, cave walls behind.

You cannot swap these scenes: one is liquid filling an open mouth, the other is solid stone hitting a ribcage. The merge itself performs the disambiguation that would otherwise need deliberate effort. (source: CAST and Georgian Node System.md)

## When to merge

Merging costs a few seconds of construction per edge, so it is the *repair and hardening* move, not a mandatory step for every edge:

- any edge scene you forget twice
- any bundle entry that keeps swapping with an adjacent one
- the two most confusable edges out of any colliding set (as in the worked example)
- every bridge edge (see [lego-skills-patterns](./lego-skills-patterns.md) — the bridge already demands Tier 2; merge it too)

Within [REMAPS](./remaps.md), merging is the **M** move (Modify / **Merge** / Move); run the full REMAPS checklist when a merged scene still feels weak. (source: CAST and Georgian Node System.md)

## Why this serves large graphs

Comprehension of a large connected graph is bounded by how many *things* the walk must hold. Merging halves the per-edge object count (three parts → one percept), and pairs with the between-scene compression of [lego-skills-patterns](./lego-skills-patterns.md) and [chunking](./chunking.md): patterns shrink the number of scenes, merging shrinks the size of each scene. Together they keep the walk perceptual — you *see* the graph rather than reciting it.

## Mnemonic

**Weld, don't stack.** Three bricks stacked fall as three; welded, they fall as one — and a missing brick leaves a visible hole. The hole *is* the alarm.
