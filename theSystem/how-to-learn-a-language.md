# How to Learn a Language

**A language is _grown_, not built.** You supply input you can _almost_
understand, and competence grows on its own. Apps that drill words and rules
(Busuu, Anki) are a side dish — they fill one 30% slot of a much larger system.
This route walks the method from "what fluency actually is" to "where the app
fits," using French zero→B2 as the worked case.

> Companion visual walkthrough: `language-learning.html` in the Neural-OS-Research
> repo (the `/explain` artifact this route was exported from).

## The one load-bearing idea

Fluent output comes from **acquired** language (built silently from input), not
from **learned** language (conscious rules you recall when you have time to
think). This is Krashen's acquisition–learning distinction. Drill builds the
learned system; only comprehensible input builds the acquired one.

## The acquisition window (i+1, the 95% rule)

Input has to be _just right_: you recognise **95–98%** of the words, so the
unknown ≤5% — the **i+1** — is inferable from context.

- below ~90% known → comprehension collapses, you flip to slow lookup-translate
- 95–98% known → the acquisition window (fastest growth)
- 100% known → comfortable, but nothing new to absorb

## The affective filter

Stress, anxiety, and self-consciousness act like a valve that _closes_ — input
arrives but never reaches the acquisition system (stress suppresses BDNF). On a
high-stress day, drop hard study and do low-stakes passive input you enjoy. A
relaxed learner acquires; a tense one just suffers.

## The daily loop: 70 / 30, volume-first

- **70%** comprehensible input (reading + listening at i+1)
- **30%** explicit encoding (this is the only slot Busuu fills)

Acquisition tracks **hours**, not number of sessions — 90 focused minutes of
input beat six 15-minute app streaks.

## One stack, ten layers — compose, don't replace

Competence is a stack: phonology, lexicon, grammar, production, pragmatics, and
more. Keep your frameworks and _add the missing layers_. Busuu covers lexicon +
some grammar; the rest — especially **phonology** at the base — it never
touches. For an English ear learning French, the highest-value first drill is
the **/y/ vs /u/** contrast (_tu_ vs _tout_), the nasals, and **/ʁ/** — the
sounds English lacks.

## Output comes later

Bias hard toward input until acquired competence can support speech, then add
low-stakes conversation. Output is the _proof_ of acquisition, not its engine —
forcing it from day one drills broken patterns under stress.

## The loci (recall route)
